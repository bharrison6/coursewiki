Motion follows unnecessary movement by a person. A labeled location may help; check the effect before adopting it.

Classify a work observation 4. A job is ready, but cannot start until missing specifications arrive. Classify the idle delay.

Waiting is idle time before work can proceed. The missing information should be investigated.

Classify a work observation 5. A station makes 40 extra pieces with no order or replenishment need. Classify the act of making extra pieces.

Overproduction means making too much or too early. It can create inventory afterward.

Classify a work observation 6. A part meets every functional and appearance requirement, but receives another unnecessary polishing pass. Classify that pass.

Over-processing adds work beyond requirements. Required finishing would be a different case.

Classify a work observation 7. A part has the wrong hole pattern and must be scrapped. Classify the failed part and its replacement work.

Defects are failures to meet requirements, leading to scrap or rework. Inspection alone is not a defective part.

Classify a work observation 8. A team never considers a trained assembler’s relevant suggestions for improving the work. Classify the unused contribution.

Skills waste means failing to use or develop people’s abilities and ideas.

Elapsed time through a sequence. One item takes 8 minutes in operation A, waits 75 minutes, then takes 12 minutes in operation B. There are no other delays or overlapping steps. Find elapsed lead time in whole minutes.

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

Lead time = 8 + 75 + 12 = 95 minutes. Process time is only 8 + 12 = 20 minutes.
