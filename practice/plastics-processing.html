A joint creeps in warmth. A part bonded with an ordinary hot-melt adhesive holds at room temperature but slips under sustained load when warm. What is the likely selection issue?

Choice 3. Hot-melt adhesive forms its own bond layer. Fast handling strength does not establish resistance to a sustained load at a higher temperature.

Count equal-height layers. A simplified print is 30 mm tall. Every layer, including the first, is 0.20 mm thick. How many layers does it have? Give a whole number.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

30 mm ÷ 0.20 mm/layer = 150 layers. This assumes constant layer height and says nothing about travel or heating time.

Estimate deposited filament mass. Use a simplified volume model: walls and skins total 6 cm³, and the remaining interior is 30 cm³ at 20% infill. Assume a density of 1.24 g/cm³. Ignore supports, purge, brim and other waste. Estimate mass in grams to the nearest 0.1 g.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

Deposited volume = 6 + (30 × 0.20) = 12 cm³. Mass = 12 × 1.24 = 14.88 g, or 14.9 g to the nearest 0.1 g. Applying 20% to the entire 36 cm³ would incorrectly reduce the solid walls and give 8.928 g. Real slicer paths need not match this ideal volume model exactly.
