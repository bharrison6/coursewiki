Read and choose dimensions

Identify the extension line. Which description identifies an extension line?

  1. The line with arrows at both ends showing a measured span.
  2. The thin line projecting a feature endpoint toward a dimension.
  3. The heavier line outlining the part.
Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

Choice 2. The extension line carries a feature endpoint out to the dimension line. The dimension line carries the arrows that show the measurement span.

Place the short dimension. A tab has a .75 width dimension and the part has a 3.60 overall width. Both dimensions are above the view. Which order is clearer?

  1. Put .75 nearer the part and 3.60 farther out.
  2. Put 3.60 nearer the part and .75 farther out.
  3. Put both dimension lines on the top edge.
Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

Choice 1. Put the short dimension nearest the part so its extension lines need not cross the overall dimension line.

Separate paper spacing from part scale. A drawing is plotted at half size (1:2). Using the 10 mm first-dimension layout guide, how far should the first dimension line be from the outline on the final sheet? Enter a whole number in mm.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

10 mm. This guide describes paper spacing, so do not halve it to 5 mm or double it to 20 mm.

Find the extra center-spacing requirement. Two holes are located from the same left edge: the first at .70 in and the second at 2.55 in. The design requires those two edge-based locations. Which additional controlling dimension repeats information?

  1. Hole diameter ⌀.28.
  2. Plate thickness .45.
  3. Center-to-center distance 1.85.
Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

Choice 3. 2.55 − .70 = 1.85 in. That spacing closes the location chain. Leave it out or show (1.85) for reference if useful. The hole diameter and plate thickness supply different information.

Read the parentheses. A spacing is shown as (2.40). What does the pair of parentheses tell the reader?

  1. The actual spacing must be exactly 2.40 with no variation.
  2. The value is reference information.
  3. The value is in millimeters even on an inch drawing.
Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

Choice 2. It is reference information. It does not impose another acceptance requirement; the actual spacing is controlled by other requirements.

Count the simple plate dimensions. A rectangular solid has one off-center circular through hole normal to its broad face. No special symmetry or other features are given. Count the independent numeric dimensions for nominal geometry: body sizes, hole size and hole location. Do not count THRU or tolerance specifications.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

6 dimensions: body length, height and thickness (3); hole diameter (1); and two center coordinates (2). THRU states the end condition. This count is for the stated ideal geometry, not a universal count for every production drawing.

Count dimensions for two equal holes. A rectangular solid has two equal-diameter through holes at unrelated positions, normal to its broad face. No symmetry or pattern spacing is given. Count body dimensions, the shared hole-size value and the four center coordinates. Do not count 2X, THRU or tolerances.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

8 dimensions: 3 for the body + 1 shared diameter + 2 coordinates for each of 2 centers = 8. A 2X note shares the size; it does not share the center location.

Choose the view for the hole diameter. A plate’s front view shows the round hole as a visible circle; its edge view shows the hole as two dashed hidden lines. Where should its diameter callout go?

  1. On the visible circle in the front view.
  2. Between the hidden lines in the edge view.
  3. On both views as separate controlling dimensions.
Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

Choice 1. The visible circular outline gives the reader the clearest connection between the leader and the round feature.

Separate the main hole from the recess. An inch drawing gives 3X ⌀.312 THRU
⌴ ⌀.625 ↧ .187. What is the main hole diameter? Give three decimal places.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

.312 in. The first diameter belongs to the main through hole. .625 is the counterbore diameter; .187 is its depth. 3X counts the repeated holes.

Read the counterbore diameter. An inch drawing gives 3X ⌀.312 THRU
⌴ ⌀.625 ↧ .187. What is the counterbore diameter? Give three decimal places.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

.625 in. Read the diameter immediately following the counterbore symbol. Do not use the .312 main-hole diameter.

Read the counterbore depth. An inch drawing gives 3X ⌀.312 THRU
⌴ ⌀.625 ↧ .187. How far below the entry surface is the counterbore floor? Give three decimal places.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

.187 in. The depth symbol follows the counterbore diameter. The main hole continues through; the recess stops at this depth.

Find the full-diameter blind depth. A hole note reads ⌀.24 ↧ .48 on an inch drawing. A pointed drill bottom is shown. What is the stated depth of the full-diameter portion? Give two decimal places.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

.48 in. The depth is to the end of the cylindrical, full-diameter portion. Do not add an assumed drill-point length to the stated value.

Read the included angle. Read ⌀.22 THRU / ⌵ ⌀.46 × 82°. What is the included countersink angle? Enter whole degrees.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

82°. This is the angle across both sides of the cone. 41° would be the half-angle, not the specified included angle.

Read the number of places. A note reads 6X ⌀.18 THRU and points to the identified hole pattern. How many holes share the stated diameter and end condition?

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

6 holes. 6X is a count, not a multiplier applied to the .18 diameter. The drawing must also locate the pattern.

Calculate slot center spacing. An original straight slot has semicircular ends, .50 in width and 1.90 in overall length. What is the distance between the end centers? Give two decimal places.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

1.40 in. Subtract the two end radii, or one full width: 1.90 − .50 = 1.40. Subtracting just one radius gives 1.65, which is incorrect. This is a geometry check, not a reason to add a third controlling dimension to the drawing.

Read a decimal-inch value. The drawing states that dimensions are in inches. A feature is labeled .625. Enter its value in inches to three decimal places.

Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

0.625 in (also written .625). The omitted leading zero follows decimal-inch drawing convention. There is no conversion to millimeters in this question.

Keep the decimal places. The same nominal length appears as .40 in one drawing and .400 in another. What must you check before treating their requirements as equivalent?

  1. Whether .400 is ten times larger.
  2. Each drawing’s applicable tolerance block and local tolerance.
  3. Whether extra zeros make the actual part mathematically exact.
Choose or enter an answer, then check it.
Show the reasoning

Choice 2. Their nominal lengths are equal, but the general tolerance blocks or local tolerances may assign different limits. No universal tolerance can be inferred from the number of decimal places alone.
