Motor choices and load points

Hold without feedback: An energized mechanism must hold at commanded steps without a feedback sensor, within its load rating. Which is the best fit? 1 = ordinary brushed DC motor alone; 2 = stepper with driver; 3 = servo system. Enter the choice number.

2: stepper with driver. It can hold a commanded step while energized. Excessive load can cause missed steps, and open-loop control cannot verify position.

3: brushless permanent-magnet motor. The electronic drive switches stator current. No brushes alone would not uniquely identify it: induction motors and steppers can also be brushless.

Speed at constant load: Assume a fixed-voltage linear torque-speed curve with stall torque 0.8 N·m and no-load speed 3,000 rpm. What steady speed results at a constant resisting load of 0.2 N·m? Give whole rpm.

n = 3,000(1 − 0.2/0.8) = 2,250 rpm. One-quarter stall torque leaves three-quarters no-load speed.

Power at the load point: Assume a fixed-voltage linear torque-speed curve with stall torque 0.8 N·m and no-load speed 3,000 rpm. Find mechanical output power at a constant 0.2 N·m load. Give watts to two decimal places; carry intermediate values to at least four significant figures.

Show worked solution

n = 3,000(1 − 0.2/0.8) = 2,250 rpm. ω = 2,250 × 2π/60 = 235.619… rad/s. P = 0.2 × 235.619… = 47.12 W. Using 235.6 rad/s also gives 47.12 W. Multiplying torque by rpm directly gives 450, which is not watts.

Speed of peak power: An ideal linear motor curve has 1.2 N·m stall torque and 3,600 rpm no-load speed. At what speed is shaft output power greatest? Give whole rpm.

Peak power occurs at half no-load speed: 3,600/2 = 1,800 rpm. At 3,600 rpm the external torque is zero, so shaft output power is zero.

Maximum shaft power: An ideal linear motor curve has 1.2 N·m stall torque and 3,600 rpm no-load speed. What is its maximum mechanical output power? Give watts to one decimal place; carry intermediate values to at least four significant figures.

At half speed and half torque: n = 1,800 rpm and T = 0.6 N·m. P = 0.6 × (1,800 × 2π/60) = 113.097… = 113.1 W. Stall torque × no-load angular speed would give 452.4 W: those endpoints cannot occur together.
