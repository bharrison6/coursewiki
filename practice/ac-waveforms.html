Zero-mean sine RMS. An ideal sine voltage has 0 V DC offset and 2.00 V amplitude, so it ranges from −2.00 to +2.00 V. What is its total RMS over one complete cycle? Give four significant figures.

For a sine with no offset, VRMS = A/√2 = 2/√2 = 1.414 V. Its signed mean is zero, but its RMS is not.

Total RMS with DC offset. A waveform is v(t) = 1.00 + 2.00 sin(2πft) volts. Its AC-component RMS is 2/√2 V. What is the total RMS including the 1.00 V offset?

VRMS,total = √(1² + (2/√2)²) = √3 = 1.732 V. Adding offset and amplitude directly would not calculate RMS.

Peak-to-peak voltage. An ideal sine has amplitude 3.00 V and a DC offset of −1.00 V. What is its peak-to-peak voltage?

Peak-to-peak is twice the sine amplitude: 2 × 3.00 = 6.00 V. The offset shifts both extrema equally and does not change their separation.
