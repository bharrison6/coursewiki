Four quantities, four ways into the table

1. Current in a control circuit: A resistor in a control circuit is 470 Ω. What current flows when 24 V is applied across it? Give the answer in milliamperes.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution
  1. Pick the form with the two quantities you have: I = V/R = 24 V ÷ 470 Ω = 0.051064 A.

Then move the prefix:

0.051064 A × 1,000 mA1 A = 51.06 mA

The current is 51.1 mA. Check the units rather than trusting the arithmetic: an ohm is a volt per ampere, so volts divided by ohms leaves amperes. And mind the case — mA is a milliampere, while MA would be a megaampere, a billion times larger.

2. Voltage drop along a cord: A portable tool draws 12 A through an extension cord. Both of the cord’s conductors are in the current path — out to the tool and back — and together they add 0.32 Ω to the circuit. What is the voltage drop across the cord?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution
  1. V = IR = 12 A × 0.32 Ω = 3.84 V.

The drop is 3.84 V, and that is 3.84 V that never reaches the tool: the supply is divided between the cord and the load, and the cord takes its share first.

Read the resistance figure carefully before using it. A cord carries the current out on one conductor and back on the other, so what belongs in this calculation is the resistance of the whole path. A table that gives resistance per unit length gives it for a single conductor; use that figure alone for a cord and the answer comes out about half of what a meter across the ends would show.

3. Resistance from a nameplate: A resistive heating element is rated 1,500 W at 120 V. What resistance does that rating imply?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution
  1. The two quantities given are power and voltage, so use R = V2/P.
  2. R = (120 V)2 ÷ 1,500 W = 14,400 ÷ 1,500 = 9.6 Ω.

The rating implies 9.6 Ω. Two steps get to the same place if you prefer: I = P/V = 12.5 A, then R = V/I = 120 ÷ 12.5 = 9.6 Ω.

This is the resistance at the rated operating point. A metal heating element is not the same resistance cold as it is hot, so a meter reading on an unpowered element will not match the nameplate figure exactly.

4. Heat lost in a cable run: A cable run carries 25 A and has a total resistance of 0.18 Ω. How much power is lost as heat in it?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution
  1. P = I2R = (25 A)2 × 0.18 Ω = 625 × 0.18 = 112.5 W.

The run loses 112.5 W as heat, spread along its whole length.

Notice the squared current. Halve the load to 12.5 A and the loss does not halve, it quarters, to 28.1 W. That is why a drop that is harmless on a light load becomes a real problem on a heavy one, and why the length and size of a conductor matter more the more current the load draws.

Scope and assumptions

These are idealized calculation checks. Each resistance is taken as constant at the stated value, and each supply as holding the stated voltage under load. P = VI is used here for direct current and purely resistive loads only; on alternating current, volts times amps gives apparent power in volt-amperes, and the real power in watts is that multiplied by the power factor.
