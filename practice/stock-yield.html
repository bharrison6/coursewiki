Count, balance and allocate

Count after excluded trim. A 1,000 mm bar has 10 mm left and 20 mm right excluded trim, each already including its own cut loss. Blanks are 100 mm long. Reserve one full 2 mm separating kerf after every blank, including the last. All blanks are acceptable and have equal cross-section. How many whole blanks fit under this policy?

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

Usable interval = 1,000 − 10 − 20 = 970 mm. Each blank plus its kerf uses 102 mm. floor(970 / 102) = 9 blanks. Ten need 1,020 usable mm. Do not add trim cut losses again.

Close the stock length balance. A 1,000 mm bar has 30 mm total excluded trim, already including trim cut losses. Nine acceptable 100 mm blanks each require a full 2 mm separating kerf, including the last blank. How long is the remaining offcut?

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

Offcut = 1,000 − 30 − 9 × 100 − 9 × 2 = 52 mm. Check: 30 + 900 + 18 + 52 = 1,000 mm. Counting only eight kerfs would incorrectly give 54 mm.

Find the next blank threshold. Blanks are 98 mm long. Reserve one full 3 mm separating kerf after every blank, including the last. With no excluded trim, what minimum stock length supplies ten whole blanks under this policy?

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

Ten blank-and-kerf intervals need 10 × (98 + 3) = 1,010 mm. At 1,009.99 mm only nine fit. The n − 1 kerf method gives 1,007 mm, which belongs to a different final-cut policy.

Allocate stock cost after a count drop. A $48 stock bar yields nine identical acceptable blanks under the stated layout. Charge the whole stock price equally to these blanks, with no remnant credit or other charges. Find stock-only cost per acceptable blank to the nearest cent.

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

$48 / 9 = $5.333… = $5.33 per blank. Continuing to divide by ten would give $4.80 and leave part of the stock charge unassigned.

Use purchased length in utilization. Nine acceptable 100 mm blanks are made from a 1,000 mm bar of the same cross-section. Excluded trims, separating kerfs and offcut are outside the blanks. What is utilization to one decimal place, based on all purchased material?

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

Acceptable blank length = 9 × 100 = 900 mm. Utilization = 900 / 1,000 × 100 = 90.0%. Dividing by only a 970 mm prepared interval would give 92.8%, a different denominator.

Interpret a zero-blank layout. A $12, 100 mm bar must supply 98 mm blanks with a full 3 mm separating kerf after every blank, including the last. There is no excluded trim. Which statement follows under this policy? Enter the choice number.

  1. One blank fits and costs $12.
  2. No blank fits, and stock cost per acceptable blank is undefined.
  3. No blank fits, so each acceptable blank costs $0.
Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

Choice 2. One blank-and-kerf interval needs 101 mm, so floor(100 / 101) = 0. The stock still costs $12, but division by zero blanks does not define a per-blank cost.
