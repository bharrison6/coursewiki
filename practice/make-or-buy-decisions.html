Relevant costs and supported decisions

Include the avoidable setup. An original hypothetical one-month order needs 100 acceptable brackets. Making requires a new $300 setup plus $9 per acceptable part, both avoided by buying. Shared rent of $600 is paid unchanged either way; a $200 study paid last month is irrecoverable. Assume spare capacity, no displaced work or alternative income, and no other route-dependent costs or credits. What is the total relevant cost of making? Give dollars to the nearest cent.

$300 + $9 × 100 = $1,200.00. The setup is relevant because this choice changes whether it is paid, even though it is fixed for the order. The unchanged rent and irrecoverable past study do not change the choice. $900 omits the setup; $1,800 includes unchanged rent; $2,000 also includes the sunk study.

Count buying charges once. An original hypothetical one-month order needs 100 acceptable brackets. Buying has a $10 per-part quote, a separate $0.50 incoming check per part and $100 freight charged once for the order. The quote excludes checks and freight. Shared rent of $600 stays unchanged; a past $200 study is irrecoverable. Assume the route delivers all 100 acceptable parts by the deadline, with no other route-dependent costs or credits. What is the total relevant buying cost? Give dollars to the nearest cent.

($10 + $0.50) × 100 + $100 = $1,150.00. The $10.50 already includes the incoming check, so adding another $50 would double count it. $1,000 is only the quote; $1,050 omits freight; $11,050 incorrectly charges freight for every part.

Separate unchanged and sunk costs. A hypothetical one-month make-or-buy order would require a new $300 setup only if made. Shared rent of $600 will be paid unchanged under either route. A $200 study paid last month cannot be recovered under either route. Which treatment belongs in a relevant-cost comparison? Enter the choice number.

  1. Exclude the setup because it is fixed; charge rent only to make.
  2. Include the new setup for make; exclude unchanged rent and the irrecoverable study from both relevant-cost totals.
  3. Include all three charges only for make because they can be assigned to the order.

Choice 2. The new setup changes with this choice; being fixed does not remove its relevance. The rent is a future cost that does not change. The study is a past irrecoverable cost. An allocation label alone does not show that a payment can be avoided.

Limit the conclusion to its evidence. A hypothetical comparison for the same 100 acceptable brackets delivered within one month gives relevant totals of $1,200 to make and $1,150 to buy. Equivalent quality and timely delivery are assumed for the calculation; spare internal capacity with no displaced work or alternative income is also assumed. Neither route's actual suitability has been verified. Which conclusion is supported? Enter the choice number.

  1. Place the supplier order now because a lower total proves acceptable quality and delivery.
  2. Make the parts because the $300 setup is fixed and must therefore be excluded from the stated making total.
  3. Buying is $50 lower under the assumptions; verify quality, capacity and deadline evidence and revise costs if those assumptions fail before committing.

Choice 3. $1,200 − $1,150 = $50. A conditional cost result does not verify a supplier. New avoidable setup belongs in the comparison. Displaced work, if discovered, may add an opportunity cost; other changed assumptions may alter either route's total.
