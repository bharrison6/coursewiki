Which ordered placement gives the positive drop across R1? 1 = red after S1, black after R1; 2 = red after R1, black after S1; 3 = both probes after S1; 4 = red supply −, black supply +.

1. Current leaves the positive source terminal, passes S1, then crosses R1. Red before R1 and black after R1 gives V(red) − V(black) = +4 V.

Reverse the probes across R1. The same closed 12 V, 30 Ω / 60 Ω series circuit has 4 V across R1. Put red after R1 and black after S1. What signed reading appears?

V(red) − V(black) = 8 V − 12 V = −4 V. A negative sign gives polarity information; it is not an error.

Measure across the only open switch. An ideal 12 V source supplies 30 Ω R1 and 60 Ω R2 in series. S1, immediately before R1, is open; S2, immediately before R2, is closed. Put red on supply + and black after S1. What signed voltage appears?

No current flows, so neither resistor has a voltage drop. The downstream side is connected to supply − through the zero-current resistors. The sole open switch therefore has +12 V across it in this ideal circuit.

Recognize a same-node reading. Both probes touch the same uninterrupted conductor after S1. What voltage does an ideal meter report?

0 V. Both probes are at the same potential, whatever that potential is relative to supply −.

Do not invent a floating-node voltage. Both series switches are open, isolating a conductor between them.
