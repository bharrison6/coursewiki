Count a free component’s motions

A rigid component floats freely in a three-dimensional assembly. It has no mates, contacts, or fixed state. How many independent degrees of freedom does it have relative to the assembly frame?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

6 DOF. Three translations and three rotations: 3 + 3 = 6. Three is the count of axes, not the count of independent rigid-body motions.

Seat a flat plate on a base

A free rigid plate receives one coincident mate between its flat underside and the top plane of a grounded base. No other mates or contact constraints exist. How many DOF remain?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

3 DOF. The plate can slide in two directions within the plane and rotate about the plane normal. The mate removes normal translation and two tilting motions: 6 − 3 = 3.

Align a shaft with a bore

A free shaft is mated concentrically to the cylindrical bore of a grounded housing. Rotation is not locked. There are no other mates or contacts. How many DOF remain?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

2 DOF. Translation along the common axis and rotation about that axis remain. The axis alignment removes four of the initial six independent motions: 6 − 4 = 2.

Seat the shaft shoulder

A shaft already has a concentric mate to a grounded bore, with rotation unlocked. Add a coincident mate between its shoulder face and a housing end face, both perpendicular to the common axis. No other constraints exist. How many DOF remain?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

1 DOF. Concentric alignment left axial slide and spin. Seating the shoulder removes the slide. Rotation about the common axis remains: 2 − 1 = 1.

Lock the remaining rotation

A shaft’s axis and shoulder are located as in the previous type of joint: only rotation about its axis remains. An independent angular constraint now fixes that remaining rotation. How many DOF remain? Assume compatible constraints.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

0 DOF. The final independent rotation is removed: 1 − 1 = 0. This is appropriate for a stationary part; a pivot meant to turn should retain that rotation.

Choose mates that fully locate a cover

A rigid rectangular cover must be stationary relative to a grounded rectangular housing. Their corresponding bottom, side, and end datum planes are mutually perpendicular and geometrically compatible. Choose A, B, or C and explain.

  • A: Coincident bottom planes only.
  • B: Coincident bottom planes, then a parallel mate between those same planes.
  • C: Coincident bottom, corresponding side, and corresponding end planes.

Self-check: write your reasoning before revealing the explanation. This text is not automatically graded or saved.

Show answer and reasoning

C. Seating the bottom leaves two in-plane slides and one spin. The independent side-plane condition removes one slide and that spin. The end-plane condition removes the last slide. A leaves three DOF. B repeats an orientation already established by coincidence. This answer assumes matching perpendicular datum planes, not arbitrary selections.

Find the redundant relationship

A shaft has a concentric mate to a grounded housing bore, plus a seated shoulder mate. Which proposed addition repeats an existing geometric condition: A, a parallel relation between those same two axes; or B, an independent constraint fixing the remaining shaft rotation? Explain.


Self-check: write your reasoning before revealing the explanation. This text is not automatically graded or saved.

Show answer and reasoning

A is redundant. Coincident axes are already parallel. B removes the remaining spin; whether it is wanted depends on whether the shaft is meant to rotate. Do not add B merely to remove an under-defined indicator.

Choose a meaningful motion check

A lever clears its housing at both travel endpoints. A mate animation moves, but you have not checked intermediate positions or collision settings. Can you conclude that it clears the housing throughout its travel? Describe the next check.


Self-check: write your reasoning before revealing the explanation. This text is not automatically graded or saved.

Show answer and reasoning

No. Inspect intermediate positions across the intended range and use appropriate interference or collision checks on the whole connected assembly. Endpoint clearance and a moving preview do not establish clearance everywhere. Check the scope of the tool: a joint preview may ignore other mates, and ordinary dragging may allow solids to pass through each other.

Recover a reference after renaming

A part was renamed in ordinary File Explorer. Its assembly now reports the old filename as missing. Choose the best response: A, suppress the missing component permanently; B, verify the intended file and repair the reference using the CAD workflow, then save and reopen affected documents; or C, insert an unrelated part with the old name.


Self-check: write your reasoning before revealing the explanation. This text is not automatically graded or saved.

Show answer and reasoning

B. Confirm the correct component identity before repairing its reference. Save the affected documents and check drawings as well as assemblies. Suppressing the component hides the symptom; substituting an unrelated file can make a model open while describing the wrong design.
