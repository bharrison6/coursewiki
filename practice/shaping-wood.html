Choose a machine for resawing

A straight board must be divided through its width into two thinner boards. Which machine is the best starting choice, assuming adequate capacity?

  1. Miter saw
  2. Band saw
  3. Thickness planer
Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

Choice 2. A band saw resaws the thickness into separate boards. A planer removes thickness as chips; it does not recover a second board.

Choose a machine for an enclosed opening

A thin panel needs a delicate enclosed curved opening, with no access cut from its outside edge. Which route fits?

  1. Band saw entering from the edge
  2. Miter saw
  3. Scroll saw with its blade threaded through a starter hole
Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

Choice 3. The scroll-saw blade can enter the enclosed region through a starter hole. A continuous band cannot be threaded through an ordinary starter hole.

Repeat an edge profile

Several flat blanks need the same curved outside outline copied from a template. Which machine and tooling best fit?

  2. Drill press with twist bit
  3. Thickness planer

Choice 1. A template supplies the path for suitable router tooling. The bit removes the wood to that path.

Choose a bit for a blind recess

A wood part needs a clean-sided blind circular recess with a largely flat bottom. A small center mark is acceptable. Which bit best fits?

  1. Hole saw
  2. Twist bit
  3. Forstner bit
Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

Choice 3. A Forstner bit removes the recess interior and leaves a largely flat bottom. A hole saw leaves a core, and an ordinary twist bit leaves a conical bottom.

Order the rough-stock milling steps

Choose the final-sizing sequence that first establishes references, then controls thickness, width and length. Any preliminary breakdown has already left the blank oversized.

  1. Plane → rip → face-joint → crosscut → edge-joint
  2. Face-joint → edge-joint → plane → rip → crosscut
  3. Crosscut to final length → rip → plane → edge-joint → face-joint
Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

Choice 2. The flat face supports thickness control; the straight square edge supports width control. Final end cuts follow the established face and edge geometry.

Recognize a planer reference

A bowed board goes through a thickness planer without a flattening fixture or a previously flattened face. What is the best prediction?

  1. It must emerge flat because thickness is uniform
  2. The planer automatically makes one edge square
  3. It may retain a bow after the rollers release it
Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

Choice 3. Roller pressure can temporarily flatten the board. Uniform thickness does not prove flatness; establish a reference face first.

Recognize bent lamination

A curved rail is made from thin strips with grain along their length, bonded together over a form. Which description fits?

  1. Bent lamination; springback can still occur
  2. Veneering; the face layer alone supplies all support
  3. Steam bending; adhesive removes the need for a form
Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

Choice 1. The strips bend separately and the cured bonds retain the assembled curve. The bend may still open after release, so form compensation needs a trial.

Count strips with kerf

A board has exactly 12 in of usable width. Each 3 in strip is removed with its own cut from a remaining offcut. Kerf is 0.125 in per cut. Ignore other allowances. What is the maximum whole number of strips?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

3 strips. floor(12 / (3 + 0.125)) = 3. Three strips and three kerfs use 9.375 in; four would need 12.5 in. Ignoring kerf gives 4, which does not fit.

Calculate the remaining offcut

From a 10 in usable width, remove three strips, each 2 in wide, using three cuts with a 0.125 in kerf. No other material is removed. What offcut width remains? Enter inches to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

3.625 in. 10 − 3(2) − 3(0.125) = 3.625. The kerfs remove 0.375 in. Ignoring them would give 4 in.

Divide a board into equal final strips

An exactly 12 in wide board is divided into four equal strips with three cuts, each with a 0.125 in kerf. Both outside edges are already finished, no offcut remains, and no further allowance is needed. How wide is each strip? Enter inches to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show answer and reasoning

2.906 in to three decimal places. (12 − 3 × 0.125) / 4 = 2.90625 in. Four pieces have three intervening cuts. Ignoring kerf gives 3 in; counting four cuts gives 2.875 in. Both are outside the answer tolerance.
