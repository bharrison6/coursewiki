GO plug will not enter

A clean, identified plain GO plug will not enter the specified inspection length of a hole using the approved method. Has this check passed? What should happen next?

Reveal reasoning

No. The GO plug should enter. Hold the part for investigation; an undersize hole is one possible cause, but form, burrs or alignment can also obstruct entry. Do not force the gauge or accept the part because the NO-GO end also stays out.

Both plug ends enter

The GO plug enters, and the NO-GO plug also enters beyond the engagement allowed by the plain-hole gauging procedure. Is the hole acceptable on this check?

Reveal reasoning

No. GO entry is required, but the NO-GO end must not enter beyond the permitted engagement. This is a failed upper-limit check; investigate and contain the affected work.

Which ring should pass?

For a plain shaft checked by an appropriate GO/NO-GO ring pair, which ring should pass over the specified length? What does a NO-GO ring passing mean?


Self-check: write your reasoning before revealing. This response is not automatically graded or saved.

Reveal reasoning

The GO ring should pass; it checks the large-shaft limit. The NO-GO ring should not pass under the specified procedure. If it passes, the shaft fails that lower-limit check. Correct gauge identity, condition and use still matter.

Is the sample centred?

Five invented readings are 19.98, 20.00, 20.00, 20.03 and 20.04 mm. The limits are 19.95 to 20.05 mm. Compare the sample mean with the limit midpoint. Does this prove capability?


Self-check: write your reasoning before revealing. This response is not automatically graded or saved.

Reveal reasoning

The mean is 20.01 mm; the midpoint is (19.95 + 20.05) / 2 = 20.00 mm. This sample is 0.01 mm above the midpoint, not exactly centred. Its readings all pass, but five readings cannot establish long-term capability.
