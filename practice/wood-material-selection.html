Wood material decisions

Explicit sales tally: A seller tallies a board using nominal thickness 1 in, measured width 7 in, and length 9 ft. Use exactly those dimensions. How many board feet? Give two decimals.

1 × 7 × 9 / 12 = 5.25 BF. This is the stated tally basis, not a measurement of the finished volume.

Each board is 1.25 × 5.5 × 8 / 12 = 4.583333… BF. Multiply the unrounded value by 3: 13.75 BF.

Material allowance: Your parts need 30 BF of physical volume. You expect 20% of the material you buy to be lost to saw kerf, defects, flattening and layout. Using that convention, how many board feet of physical volume should you buy? Give two decimals.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

The 20% is a share of what you buy, so only 80% of the purchase becomes parts: 30 ÷ (1 − 0.20) = 30 ÷ 0.80 = 37.50 BF. Adding 20% to the need (30 × 1.20 = 36 BF) uses the other convention and leaves you short under this one.

All dimensions in inches: A piece actually measures 2 in × 9 in × 40 in. Find its physical volume in board feet to two decimals.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

All dimensions are inches, so divide cubic inches by 144: 2 × 9 × 40 / 144 = 5.00 BF.

Painted interior panel: Choose a smooth panel for a nonstructural painted interior decorative door with a routed profile, in dry service. 1 = ordinary MDF, 2 = rough structural OSB. Enter the better starting choice.

1 — MDF. Fine fibers provide a smooth surface and consistent profiles. Plan suitable fastening; this does not make ordinary MDF a wet-service product.
