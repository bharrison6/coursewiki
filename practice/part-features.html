Choose and check part features

Choose a base feature. A round knob has several coaxial outside steps and a central bore. Which base best keeps those radial and axial dimensions in one section? Choose 1 = revolve, 2 = shell, 3 = linear pattern.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

1: Revolve. A section can describe the steps and bore together. This is a design-intent choice; extrusions could also construct the shape.

Keep a cut through. A hole must pass through a single plate even when the plate thickness increases. Choose 1 = blind at the current thickness, 2 = through all in the cutting direction, 3 = blind at half the thickness.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

2: Through all expresses the requirement. A blind cut tied only to the old thickness may leave a floor.

Split a total depth. A SOLIDWORKS Mid Plane extrusion has a total depth of 18 mm. How far does it extend from the sketch plane on each side?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

18 ÷ 2 = 9.00 mm on each side. Using 18 mm on each side would double the total depth.

Count a minimum with stated limits. Starting with no solid, make a plain rectangular block with one circular through hole. Allow only ordinary straight boss extrusions from closed sketch regions, including nested loops. Count solid features only, not sketches. What is the minimum number of solid features?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

1 feature. Extrude a rectangular region with a circular region excluded all the way through. A separate boss and cut uses 2 features and may express independent design intent better, but is not the minimum under these rules.

Find a lost reference. The tree is: plate extrusion; hole using a sketch on its top face; pattern using that hole; an unrelated edge chamfer. Delete only the hole while retaining the plate. Which feature definitely loses its stated input? Choose 1 = plate extrusion, 2 = hole pattern, 3 = every earlier feature.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

2: The hole pattern loses its seed. Deletion dialogs may offer dependent deletions. Do not infer that every later feature must fail merely because it appears later.

Estimate mass from volume. A uniform solid spacer is 24 × 15 × 10 mm with no holes. Use the assumed density 2.50 g/cm³. What is its mass in grams? Give two decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

Volume = 24 × 15 × 10 = 3,600 mm³ = 3.6 cm³. Mass = 3.6 × 2.50 = 9.00 g. There are 1,000 mm³ in 1 cm³; multiplying 3,600 directly by the density would give a result 1,000 times too large.

Recognize a profile and path. You have a circular section and a smooth bent centerline. You want a solid rod of constant circular size along that line. Choose the simplest starting feature: 1 = chamfer, 2 = sweep, 3 = shell.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

2: Sweep the circular profile along the path. Verify profile alignment and enough bend radius to avoid self-intersection.

Connect changing sections. You have a large oval section, a medium oval section, and a small oval section on three separated planes. Which feature directly transitions through these ordered sections? Choose 1 = linear pattern, 2 = fillet, 3 = loft.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

3: Loft. Select the profiles in spatial order and inspect the connections. Add guides only when needed to control the shape between sections.

Count the spaces in a row. A single-direction linear pattern has 5 total hole instances including the seed, with 12 mm between adjacent centers. No instances are skipped. What is the first-to-last center distance?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

There are 5 − 1 = 4 intervals. Span = 4 × 12 = 48.00 mm. Multiplying by 5 counts one interval too many.

Space a full-circle pattern. Eight total holes, including the seed, are evenly spaced around a complete circle, with no duplicate at 360°. What is the angular pitch between adjacent holes?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

360° ÷ 8 = 45.00°. The last-to-first gap is also an interval; dividing by 7 is for a different endpoint-inclusive arc construction.

Choose symmetry. A tab must have an oppositely handed counterpart at equal distance across a center plane. Choose 1 = mirror, 2 = translate with a linear pattern, 3 = shell.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

1: Mirror reflects the tab. Translation preserves handedness and does not encode plane symmetry.

Hollow a solid. A solid block must become an open container with a controlled wall and floor thickness. Choose 1 = fillet, 2 = shell with the top face removed, 3 = circular pattern.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

2: Shell. Select the opening face, thickness, and inward or outward direction, then inspect the result in section.

Add a support web. You have a sketch line spanning two supporting walls and need a thin reinforcing web with controlled thickness. Choose 1 = chamfer, 2 = shell, 3 = rib.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

3: Rib. The sketch, thickness, direction, and target part define the support. Check that the web joins the walls.

Tilt faces for release. Side faces must taper relative to a pull direction while the neutral-plane section stays fixed. Choose 1 = draft, 2 = mirror, 3 = circular pattern.

Show reasoning

1: Draft. Check which end grows or shrinks. The required angle depends on the design and process; it is not supplied by this question.

Choose a cylindrical recess. A hole needs a larger cylindrical recess with a flat shoulder above its smaller bore. Choose 1 = countersink, 2 = counterbore, 3 = plain hole with no recess.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

2: Counterbore. A countersink has a conical recess. Define the type and size separately from the center position.

Distinguish bevel from radius. A sharp outside edge must become a straight bevel in section, rather than a rounded transition. Choose 1 = chamfer, 2 = fillet, 3 = rib.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show reasoning

1: Chamfer. A fillet gives a curved transition; a rib adds a support web.
