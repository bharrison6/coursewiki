Identify a radial load. A force acts vertically downward on a horizontal shaft. The shaft axis is horizontal. Which load is this?

Answer 1. The force is perpendicular to the shaft axis, so it is radial.

Recognize combined loading. A shaft carries a sideways belt pull and a separate force along its axis. What must its complete support arrangement carry?

Answer 2. The two perpendicular components make combined loading. They may be assigned to different bearings.

Select for repeated starts. A small shaft repeatedly starts and runs at moderate speed under radial load and modest thrust in either direction. Which is a sensible first catalog candidate if low starting resistance matters?

Answer 3. A deep-groove ball bearing can carry radial load and thrust in both directions with generally low starting resistance. Final selection still needs speed, load, life and lubrication checks.

Answer 2. Fluid-film bearings serve high-speed machinery. Their design and oil conditions matter; the category name is not a speed limit.

Find the locating support. Support A retains both rings axially and is rated for radial load and thrust both ways. Support B carries radial load while allowing axial travel. Which end locates the shaft?

Answer 1. A provides axial location and carries the applied thrust. B accommodates relative axial growth.

Predict exhausted floating travel. The shaft wants more thermal growth than the floating seat has travel. What happens in the ideal model?

Choose an answer, then check it.

Answer 3. Floating works only while sufficient travel remains. After the limit is reached, the support arrangement resists further expansion.

Count round-shaft freedoms. In the ideal geometric model, an adequately supported round sleeve has no anti-rotation key or axial stop. How many rigid-body freedoms remain? This is not a rotary service rating.

Answer 2. Translation and rotation about the shaft remain: two freedoms. Four are restrained. Ball-bushing rotary service still requires a suitable product rating.

Count profile-guide freedoms. A properly engaged profile carriage supports its rated transverse forces and moments. Which intended motion remains?

Answer 3. The profile geometry restrains the other five ideal rigid-body motions.

Answer 1. A shim sets distance in the stack. The assembly geometry determines how its thickness changes end play.

Maintain preload over small movement. An arrangement calls for a specified preload while allowing small dimensional changes. Which approach is appropriate?

Answer 2. A properly selected wave or Belleville spring applies force over a working deflection range. The required preload is a specification, not a feel test.

Calculate free shaft growth. A 600 mm carbon-steel shaft warms uniformly by 40 °C and is free to expand. Use ΔL = α·L·ΔT, where L is the original length and ΔT is the temperature rise. For this estimate, use a constant α = 10.44 × 10−6 per °C. How much does the shaft length increase, in mm? Keep at least four significant figures in calculations and report four significant figures.

Enter an answer, then check it.
Show worked solution

The DOE Fundamentals Handbook, Material Science, Volume 2, Table 1 (MS-03, page 2) lists carbon steel at 5.8 × 10−6 per °F. A temperature rise of 1 °C equals a rise of 1.8 °F, so α = 5.8 × 10−6 × 1.8 = 10.44 × 10−6 per °C.

ΔL = (10.44 × 10−6 /°C)(600 mm)(40 °C) = 0.25056 mm ≈ 0.2506 mm. Using mm for L gives mm for ΔL. Four significant figures describe the arithmetic, not the accuracy of this approximate material coefficient.
