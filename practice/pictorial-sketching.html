Sketch a circle on an isometric top face. A circular feature lies on the horizontal top face of an isometric block. What should its sketch look like?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

The face is tilted to the picture plane, so the circle appears elliptical. The four midpoints locate tangencies; joining them with straight lines does not form the curve.

Match a beveled block to its top view. Which candidate is the top view? The front of the part is at the bottom of every candidate.

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Candidate 2. The bevel runs through the entire depth and begins two-thirds of the way across the width from the left. Its boundary therefore runs front to back near the right side. Candidate 1 changes along depth; candidate 3 puts the boundary on the wrong side.

Classify sloping face B. B appears as an edge in front and a foreshortened area in top and right. What type of flat surface is it?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Inclined. B is perpendicular to the front projection plane and tilted to the other two. The sloping line in front is its edge view.

Recognize an oblique surface. A flat face is tilted to all three principal planes, parallel to none and perpendicular to none. Which statement is correct?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

An oblique surface shows an area in all three principal views, but none is its true shape. This describes surface orientation, not the pictorial method.

Check before extruding a profile. You have recognized the front profile. What must you check before carrying it unchanged through the full depth?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Top and side must support a constant cross-section. A pocket or step may end partway through the depth; the front outline alone cannot establish that. A matching overall width and height confirms only the enclosing box.
