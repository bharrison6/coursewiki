Synchronize separated shafts

1. Synchronize separated shafts: Two separated parallel shafts must stay synchronized in a clean area where oil at the drive contact is unwanted. Which ordinary drive is the best starting candidate? 1 = flat belt; 2 = timing belt; 3 = lubricated roller chain; 4 = a single compact gear pair.

Show worked solution

2 — timing belt. Teeth maintain timing and the belt contact runs dry. A flat belt can slip; ordinary chain needs joint lubrication; a compact gear pair cannot bridge the stated gap. This is a shortlist choice, followed by load and environment checks.

Quiet fan drive on a budget

2. Quiet fan drive on a budget: A sheltered fan has separated parallel shafts. Low initial cost and quiet running matter, and a little speed variation is acceptable. Which is the best starting candidate? 1 = V-belt; 2 = precision helical gearbox with extra shafts; 3 = exposed roller chain; 4 = precision timing drive solely to guarantee synchronization.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

1 — V-belt. The job accepts the speed variation of a friction drive and benefits from a simple, quiet flexible span. The other choices add requirements or costs the scenario does not call for. Check the actual power and pulley ratings before selection.

Match an existing chain interface

3. Match an existing chain interface: A sheltered low-speed conveyor has serviceable ANSI #40 sprockets, suitable lubrication access and no requirement for quiet precision positioning. Which replacement transmission fits these specified wheels? 1 = flat belt; 2 = V-belt; 3 = compatible #40 roller chain; 4 = 08B chain chosen only because its pitch is also 12.7 mm.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

3 — compatible #40 roller chain. Match chain series and dimensions as well as pitch. Equal pitch alone does not make 08B interchangeable with #40. Confirm the strand count, load rating and connecting-link type too.

Quieter drive in a compact housing

4. Quieter drive in a compact housing: A compact lubricated gearbox has close parallel shafts and needs smoother high-speed engagement than a comparable spur pair. The bearing arrangement can carry axial thrust. Which candidate best addresses that requirement? 1 = a spur pair selected only for zero axial thrust; 2 = a slipping flat belt; 3 = a long exposed chain; 4 = a matched helical pair.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

4 — a matched helical pair. Progressive engagement generally improves smoothness and noise. The stated bearing capability addresses its axial thrust, but the tooth and bearing ratings still need checking.

Look up 08B pitch

8. Look up 08B pitch: A B-series lookup lists 05B-1 at 8.000 mm, 06B-1 at 9.525 mm, 08B-1 at 12.700 mm and 10B-1 at 15.875 mm. What pitch should a student record for 08B-1, to two decimal places in millimeters?

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

The 08B-1 row gives 12.70 mm. For this size, 8/16 in is 0.5 in, and 0.5 × 25.4 = 12.70 mm. Do not read 08 as 8 mm, and do not generalize this fraction rule to small sizes such as 05B.

Find driven-sprocket speed

10. Find driven-sprocket speed: A 21-tooth sprocket at 980 rpm drives a 49-tooth sprocket through correctly engaged chain. What is the average driven speed? Round to the nearest rpm; ignore chordal speed ripple.

nout = 980 × 21/49 = 420 rpm. The answer is 420 rpm. A larger driven sprocket turns slower. The inverse calculation would give about 2287 rpm, which fails that direction check. See [[gear-ratios]].

Find timing-pulley speed

11. Find timing-pulley speed: A timing belt connects a 28-tooth driver at 1440 rpm to a 60-tooth driven pulley. The belt and pulleys match, with no tooth jumping. What is the output speed, rounded to the nearest rpm?

nout = 1440 × 28/60 = 672 rpm. The answer is 672 rpm. Teeth, not outside pulley diameters, establish this ratio. See [[gear-ratios]].

Estimate V-belt output speed

12. Estimate V-belt output speed: A V-belt driver has an effective diameter of 90 mm and runs at 1680 rpm. The driven sheave has an effective diameter of 210 mm. Estimate output speed to the nearest rpm, explicitly assuming no slip.

Enter a number, then check it.

nout = 1680 × 90/210 = 720 rpm. The no-slip estimate is 720 rpm. It is not a guarantee of actual friction-belt speed under load. Both dimensions use the same effective-diameter convention. See [[gear-ratios]].
