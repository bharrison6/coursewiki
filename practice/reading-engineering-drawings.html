Read the shape and its drawing language

Recognize a concealed edge. An edge is blocked by material in this view. Which line represents it?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Thin, evenly spaced short dashes. Short dashes identify a hidden edge. A center line locates an axis; a visible line shows an edge you can see.

Recognize a visible edge. Which line would you read as an edge directly visible from this viewing direction?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Thick continuous line. A relatively thick continuous line is a visible or object line.

Distinguish an axis from an edge. A thin long–short line passes through the center of a circular hole. What does it identify?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

The hole center or axis. The center line locates the hole center or axis. It is not a physical edge.

Recognize an alternate position. A lever outline is repeated in a thin long–short–short pattern beside its solid outline. What is the likely meaning?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

An alternate lever position. Phantom lines can show an alternate position. Use nearby notes to confirm what the alternate outline represents.

Read a dimension line. A thin line has arrowheads at both ends and the value 42 between them. What kind of line is it?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Dimension. The dimension line identifies the extent of the stated measurement. Read the value and the drawing units rather than measuring the image.

Read an extension line. Thin lines start just outside two part edges and extend to the ends of a dimension line. What are they?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Extension lines. Extension lines carry feature locations to the dimension line. The small gap helps distinguish them from part edges.

Follow a note to its feature. A thin bent line joins a hole note to an arrow touching the hole outline. What is the line?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Leader. A leader connects the note to the feature it describes. Follow its endpoint before applying the note.

Read cutting-plane arrows. A prominent line across a view has arrows and the letter B at both ends. A separate view is labeled SECTION B–B. What do the arrows tell you?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

The direction to look into the section. The arrows set the viewing direction for the imaginary cut. B–B connects the cut location to its section view.

Read section hatching. Thin diagonal strokes fill a region in a section view. What do they ordinarily show?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Material crossed by the imaginary cut. Hatching identifies cut material. It does not instruct someone to machine diagonal grooves.

Read a shortened view. A long uniform bar is drawn with zigzag break lines and an overall length dimension. How should you interpret it?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Part of the view is omitted; read the full length dimension. A break can shorten a long view on the sheet. It does not by itself mean the physical bar is fractured.

Resolve coincident line types. A visible edge and a hidden edge project to exactly the same segment. Which line should you expect there?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Visible line. The visible line takes precedence over the hidden line at the overlap. Consult another view to recover the concealed geometry.

Identify the front view. Which candidate is the front view of the pictured part? The numbered candidates are laid out as choices, not as a projection arrangement.

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

View 2. View 2 follows the tall-left stepped profile. Width is horizontal and height is vertical.

Identify the top view. Which candidate is the top view of the pictured part? The numbered candidates are laid out as choices, not as a projection arrangement.

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

View 3. View 3 shows the rectangular footprint, split at the width where the height changes. Looking down removes height from the outline.

Identify the right-side view. Which candidate is the right view of the pictured part? The numbered candidates are laid out as choices, not as a projection arrangement.

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

View 1. View 1 shows depth and height. The near low shelf gives the visible horizontal edge; the tall strip behind it reaches the top outline.

Follow a dimension between views. Which dimension is shared by the front and top views at the same scale?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Width. Front shows width and height; top shows width and depth. Width is their shared direction.

Recognize a face seen edge-on. You look exactly along a flat face so that the face is edge-on to you. How does that face project?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

As a line. An edge-on face collapses to a line. Its full area appears only when the viewing direction exposes the face.

Decide whether a plate needs another view. A flat plate has a fully specified face outline and holes. A note states its uniform thickness; there are no other features. Must you add two more views just to reach three?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

No, a complete one-view description can be enough. The target is an unambiguous definition. Under the stated assumptions, a face view plus thickness information can define the shape.

Find information missing from one view. Two solid blocks have identical front outlines but different depths. Which added view directly distinguishes their depths?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

A top view at the same scale. The top view shows depth. Repeating or enlarging the front view does not reveal it.

Place a right view in first-angle. The drawing declares first-angle projection. Where is the right-side view normally placed relative to the front?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

To the left. First-angle puts the right-side view to the left of the front. The name still identifies the direction from which you look.

Place a top view in third-angle. The drawing declares third-angle projection. Where is the top view normally placed relative to the front?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Above. Third-angle places the top above the front. Front and top remain aligned by width.

Confirm the drawing convention. A drawing from a U.S. supplier has a clearly marked first-angle symbol. What should you do when interpreting the layout?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Use the declared first-angle convention and check view consistency. The explicit convention controls interpretation. A country or unit system is not a substitute for the symbol.
