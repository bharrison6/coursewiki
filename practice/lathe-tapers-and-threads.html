Diameter change per inch. A taper changes from 1.250 to 0.950 in over an axial length of 4.000 in. Find taper per inch to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

(1.250 − 0.950)/4.000 = 0.075 in/in. Use diameter change, not radius change.

Rounded answer: 0.075 in/in.

Convert taper per inch to per foot. A taper rate is 0.075 in/in. Express it in in/ft to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

12 × 0.075 = 0.900 in/ft. A foot contains twelve inches.

Rounded answer: 0.900 in/ft.

Convert taper per foot to per inch. A taper is specified as 0.600 in/ft. Find taper per inch to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

0.600/12 = 0.050 in/in. Do not substitute 0.600 directly into a formula that requires in/in.

Rounded answer: 0.050 in/in.

Compound taper half-angle. A taper has a diameter change of 0.080 in per inch of axial length. Find the compound travel angle from the spindle axis, to three decimal places in degrees.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

α = atan(0.080/2) = 2.291 degrees. The included angle is about 4.581 degrees; that is not the travel angle.

Rounded answer: 2.291 degrees.

Offset with unequal total and taper lengths. A shallow external taper changes from 1.200 to 1.000 in over l = 3.000 in. Total effective center-support length L is 9.000 in. Use the small-angle offset estimate and round to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

Taper rate = 0.200/3.000 in/in. Offset ≈ (0.200/3.000) × 9.000/2 = 0.300 in. Using only the taper length gives 0.100 in and is wrong.

Rounded answer: 0.300 in.

Offset from a per-foot specification. A taper is 0.720 in/ft and the effective center-support length is 8.000 in. Find the small-angle tailstock offset estimate, to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

TPI = 0.720/12 = 0.060 in/in. Offset ≈ 0.060 × 8.000/2 = 0.240 in. Treating TPF as TPI would give 2.880 in.

Rounded answer: 0.240 in.

Pitch from threads per inch. A single-start thread has 16 threads per inch. Find its axial pitch to four decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

P = 1/16 = 0.0625 in. Lead equals pitch for this single-start thread.

Rounded answer: 0.0625 in.

Lead of a two-start thread. A two-start thread has axial pitch P = 0.075 in. Find the required axial travel along one helix per revolution, to three decimal places.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

Lead = 2P = 2 × 0.075 = 0.150 in/rev. Using pitch alone gives half the required travel.

Rounded answer: 0.150 in/rev.

Ideal sharp-V radial depth. For an ideal sharp-V 60-degree teaching profile with 20 threads per inch, calculate total radial depth H = (√3/2)P. Round to five decimal places. This is a geometric model, not a finished standard thread or a per-pass setting.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

P = 1/20 = 0.050 in. H = (√3/2) × 0.050 = 0.04330 in. Diameter reduction would be 2H, not H. Real thread flats and root shape change the depth.

Rounded answer: 0.04330 in.

Ideal compound travel from radial depth. For the ideal sharp-V 60-degree, 20-per-inch teaching profile, H = (√3/2)/20 in. Find total actual compound travel at 29 degrees from radial using c = H/cos(29°). Round to five decimal places. Ignore tip truncation and backlash; this is not a production setting.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

H = 0.04330127 in. c = H/cos(29°) = 0.04951 in. Compound travel is longer than its radial component; do not double H or use H alone.

Rounded answer: 0.04951 in.
