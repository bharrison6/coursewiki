Check units and drawing language

Mass from volume and density. For this original general skill check, assume a uniform aluminum solid has volume 12.0 cm³ and density 2.70 g/cm³. Calculate its mass in grams, rounded to the nearest 0.1 g.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

m = density × volume = 2.70 g/cm³ × 12.0 cm³ = 32.4 g. Cubic centimeters cancel. Dividing volume by density gives about 4.44 and does not give units of mass.

Convert a cubic measurement. In this original general skill check, a solid's volume is 8,750 mm³. Express that same volume in cm³ to the nearest 0.01 cm³.

Show worked solution

1 cm³ = (10 mm)³ = 1,000 mm³. Therefore 8,750 ÷ 1,000 = 8.75 cm³. Dividing by 10 only, as for length, would give 875, which is wrong for volume.

Change only the displayed units. In this original general skill check, you change a CAD measurement display from millimeters to centimeters without editing geometry or material. What happens to the physical part's mass?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

It stays the same. A display conversion describes the same geometry using different units. Resizing the geometry or changing density would be a different operation.

Read a radius before entering a diameter. This original general skill check describes a circle with radius 7.0 mm. A sketch tool asks for diameter. Enter the diameter in mm to the nearest 0.1 mm.

d = 2r = 2 × 7.0 mm = 14.0 mm. Entering 7.0 as the diameter makes the circle half the intended diameter.

Recognize an enlarged detail. In this original general skill check, a drawing enlarges a small region solely to make its features easier to read. Which term describes that view?

Choose an answer, then check it.
Show explanation

Detail view. Enlarging a selected region makes small features readable. A section reveals features at an imaginary cut; an auxiliary view establishes a viewing direction perpendicular to an inclined surface.
