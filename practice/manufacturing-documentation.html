Order a supported drilling route

A small wooden tag will be drilled while attached to a larger strip that provides the required holding area. Its final cut preserves the reference edge. Put these operations in order: A — final sanding and inspection; B — cut the tag free; C — establish the reference edge and mark the hole; D — drill and inspect the hole while the strip is held in the qualified setup.

Self-check: write your answer, then compare below. This response is not automatically graded or saved.

Show a sensible order

C → D → B → A. Establish the reference before locating the hole. The problem specifies holding on the larger strip, so drill before cutting free. Finish and inspect after the cuts. This order depends on the stated setup; it is not a universal rule for drilling.

Find the missing acceptance criterion

A row says: “Drill two through holes using a drill press, the specified bit, backing board and clamps. Use the qualified setup and its recorded speed. Make the holes look good.” What inspection information is missing?

Self-check; your response is not automatically graded or saved.

Show what must be added

“Look good” is not an acceptance criterion. Add the drawing revision, hole-diameter and location limits, any required edge condition, and inspection tools or a defined comparison method. Do not invent the limits; take them from the part requirements.

Find the missing equipment and setup

A row says: “Make a 30 ±0.5 mm-wide strip. Measure width with calipers at both ends.” It identifies material and drawing revision elsewhere. Why can another person still not repeat the operation from this row?

Self-check; your response is not automatically graded or saved.

Show the missing fields

The row gives a result and an inspection method, but no equipment or route. Add the operation, equipment, tool, reference edge, support and holding method, and the required process settings with units or a qualified setup reference. Equipment selection depends on the material and available approved process.

Choose a dimension from function

A cover must slide over two fixed locating pins. Its decorative outer border has plenty of clearance. Which features would you investigate first when choosing critical dimensions, and why?

Self-check; your response is not automatically graded or saved.

Show the functional choice

Start with the hole diameters and their locations relative to the pins and each other. Those control whether the cover fits. The border still needs dimensions, but a tighter border tolerance does not fix misaligned holes. Derive limits from the mating geometry instead of simply choosing a small number.

Allocate sheet material cost

A sheet costs $35. A part is allocated 180 × 100 mm of a 1000 × 400 mm sheet of the same material and thickness. This allocation already includes cutting waste. What is the material cost per part? Report dollars to the nearest cent.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

Share = (180 × 100) / (1000 × 400) = 0.045. Cost = 0.045 × $35 = $1.575, or $1.58 to the nearest cent. Using the length fraction alone gives $6.30 and ignores the width fraction.

Extend BOM quantities once

An invented small stand uses two made side pieces at $0.85 in allocated material each and four purchased pads at $0.30 each. What is the material and component total for one stand, to the nearest cent? Exclude labor and other costs.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

2 × $0.85 + 4 × $0.30 = $1.70 + $1.20 = $2.90. Adding only the two unit prices gives $1.15 and misses the quantities.

Split a total silicone mass

For this arithmetic exercise, a silicone's stated A:B ratio is 10:1 by weight. You need 330 g total of A and B combined. How much B is needed? Report grams to one decimal place. This ratio is not a recommendation for another product.

Enter a number, then check it.
Show worked solution

There are 10 + 1 = 11 ratio parts. B = 330 / 11 = 30.0 g; A = 300.0 g. Check: 300 + 30 = 330 g. Dividing the total by 10 gives 33 g, which treats the total as A alone.

Add B to a measured mass of A

B = 275 / 10 = 27.5 g. Total mixed mass is 302.5 g. Dividing by 11 gives 25 g and incorrectly treats the given A mass as the total.
