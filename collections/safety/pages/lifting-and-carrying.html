Lifting Rules

Get a second person for anything over 40 lbs. A gym weight plate is 45 lbs and nobody lifts one in pairs. Size — not weight — is what makes shop materials hard to carry.

Forty pounds of aluminum bar is about four inches square and two feet long: you can hold it against your chest, see over it, and set it down where you meant to. A twenty-foot piece of tube can weigh less than that and still put half its length out in front of you, and a sheet of plywood fills your arms and hides the floor you are walking on. Same weight, different job.

OSHA sets no weight limit — no standard says how much a person may lift, only that a workplace be kept free of recognized hazards. The number everyone borrows comes from NIOSH, the federal institute that researches workplace injury: 51 lbs with two hands under ideal conditions, cut for every way a real lift is worse. Hold that same load at arm's length instead of against you and it rates about 20 lbs. Turning a quarter circle as you lift takes off another 30%, and nothing good to grab takes off 10% more. A load much wider than your body is outside what the equation covers at all — used there, it underestimates the hazard.

Team lifting is required for

  • Anything over 40 lbs.
  • Items lighter than 40 lbs but awkward in shape, size or balance.
  • Any object that feels unstable or is difficult to grip.

The 40 lb line is as much about dropping it as about your back. A load you cannot fully control is a hazard to your feet, the floor, the workpiece and anyone walking past — well before it is a hazard to your spine.

If you cannot hold it against your body and keep your back straight, it is too heavy for you to lift alone — whatever the number says. Weight still wins in the end; that point just sits somewhere different for every person and every shape.

Proper lifting technique

  • Plan the lift — clear your path and know where you are going.
  • Get close to the object before lifting.
  • Squat down using your leg muscles; keep your back straight.
  • Grip firmly with both hands; avoid awkward hand positions.
  • Lift smoothly using your legs, not your back.
  • Keep the load close to your body throughout the lift.

Setting it down

  • Use the same technique in reverse — squat down, do not bend over.
  • Place objects gently. Do not drop them.
