Use TIMWOODS to name what you observe

Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Overproduction, Over-processing, Defects and Skills form TIMWOODS. Describe the observed problem before choosing a remedy.

[[img:lean-flow/timwoods.svg|Eight-cell TIMWOODS grid: Transportation, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Overproduction, Over-processing, Defects and Skills.]]

Compare shop examples with everyday examples

These are illustrative examples, not descriptions of a particular shop.

Transportation: unnecessary material movement

Shop: A part travels to a distant holding area and back.
Daily life: Moving stored boxes between rooms without using them.

Inventory: excess material or work

Shop: Unneeded blanks fill a rack.
Daily life: Buying food that expires before use.

Motion: unnecessary movement by people

Shop: An operator searches several drawers for a tool.
Daily life: Searching for misplaced keys.

Waiting: idle time before work can proceed

Shop: A prepared job waits for missing information.
Daily life: A completed form sits awaiting approval.

Overproduction: too much or too early

Shop: Extra parts are made without demand.
Daily life: Printing copies nobody requested.

Over-processing: work beyond requirements

Shop: A hidden face receives an unnecessary cosmetic finish.
Daily life: Entering the same information into duplicate lists.

Defects: work that fails requirements

Shop: An incorrect hole pattern requires a replacement part.
Daily life: Retyping a form because of incorrect entries.

Skills: unused talent

Shop: An experienced operator's improvement idea is never considered.
Daily life: Ignoring a team member's relevant skill when dividing work.

Distinguish similar wastes before proposing a change

Transportation follows material; motion follows people. Overproduction creates unwanted output; inventory is the resulting excess stock. Several wastes can occur together.

Inspection is not automatically a defect, and every machine cycle is not automatically wasted time. Check [[lean-value-and-flow]]. Buffers may be needed for supply variation; removing them without understanding demand and reliability can interrupt delivery.

Use [[5s-workplace-organization]] to address searching and [[kaizen-and-standard-work]] to test a change. Improve the system rather than blaming the person caught in it.
