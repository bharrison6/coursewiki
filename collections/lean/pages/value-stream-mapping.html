Map one product family from request to delivery

A value stream map (VSM) shows the steps and information that move a product or service toward the customer. Define the boundary and select work following a similar route. Walk the current process and record what happens, including queues and handoffs.

Show who requests work, how instructions reach production, where material moves and where it waits. An operations-only map misses the information controlling those operations.

Read process boxes, inventory triangles and flow arrows

[[img:lean-flow/value-stream.svg|Simplified map: customer order goes to planning, which instructs cutting. Material passes through cutting, inventory and assembly to the customer. Cutting takes 4 minutes, waiting 120 minutes and assembly 6 minutes.]]

This original sketch uses common conventions: a rectangle for a process, a triangle marked I for inventory, attached data boxes for measurements and arrows for flow. The legend distinguishes material movement from information. This is a teaching sketch, not a complete symbol standard.

Measure elapsed lead time separately from process time

Lead time is elapsed time through the stated boundary, including waits. Process time is time actively spent doing the work within that boundary. Not all processing adds customer value.

For one item in this simplified sequential example: cutting = 4 min, queue = 120 min, assembly = 6 min. Process time is 10 min; lead time is 130 min. No other delays or overlapping steps are included. Removing half the queue saves more elapsed time here than cutting one minute of processing.

A queue suggests a question, not a proven cause

A queue may reflect a capacity constraint, batching, missing information or an unreliable next step. Observe demand, timing and variation before calling one operation the bottleneck. A working day and a calendar day are not interchangeable time units.

Draw a future state and test a specific change

A future-state map describes an improved flow to work toward. Choose a measurable change, assign responsibility and check the effect using [[kaizen-and-standard-work]]. Compare delivery, quality and workload across the whole stream.
