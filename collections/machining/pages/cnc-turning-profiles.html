A rounded tool nose does not cut at the imaginary sharp tip

A turning insert has a rounded corner, called its nose radius. Touching off along X and Z establishes an imaginary tip: the intersection of the two tangent reference lines. There is no cutting material at that intersection. The actual contact point moves around the nose as the direction of the surface changes.

Axis-parallel cuts can reach the measured diameter or face with the corresponding touch-offs. On a taper, chamfer or arc, simply driving the imaginary tip along the desired surface leaves a profile error. Account for the nose radius with a calculated toolpath or the control's tool nose compensation. See [[lathe-tapers]] for taper geometry.

[[img:cnc-turning/nose-radius.svg|A rounded nose touches the X and Z reference lines, whose intersection is the imaginary tip. A taper through that tip misses the rounded edge; a parallel tangent line shows the resulting surface without compensation.]]

Why a good diameter measurement does not prove a taper is correct

The two axis touch-offs locate tangent lines, not every possible contact point on the nose. The normal to an angled surface points in another direction, so its tangency requires another offset. Haas's tool nose compensation discussion describes the resulting undercut or overcut on tapered and curved profiles.

Compensation needs nose radius, tip orientation and a clear entry and exit

In common Fanuc-style usage, G41 selects compensation to the left of the programmed direction of travel and G42 to the right; G40 cancels it. Judge the side in the control's XZ-plane convention. Do not choose a code from the screen's apparent left or right, or assume one code means every outside cut.

Enter the actual nose radius and the control's tip orientation, which tells it where the imaginary tip sits relative to the nose centre. Select the correct tool-offset register, use the manual's lead-in and lead-out requirements, and check the resulting path with [[cnc-program-proving]]. Tip numbers differ by control; there is no universal number table here.

Do not apply full nose-radius compensation again to a path already corrected for that radius. Check whether a CAM post expects full geometry compensation, wear-only compensation or no control compensation.

Where to check the control behavior

The Haas G41/G42 entry connects the compensation side to the selected tool offsets. Its archived SL manual, Tool Nose Compensation, explains imaginary-tip direction and approach/depart moves. Use the current manual for the installed control when programming.

A roughing cycle removes layers while leaving stock for finishing

A G71-type roughing cycle uses a defined final profile to generate repeated stock-removal passes, commonly along Z for outside or inside turning. The programmer supplies the applicable cut depth, retract and finish allowance. A G70-type finishing cycle follows the defined profile to remove the remaining allowance.

[[img:cnc-turning/roughing.svg|Upper half of turning stock: successive roughing passes run along Z toward the headstock and leave an allowance above a stepped finished profile. Finishing follows the final outline.]]

Read the control's cycle definition before entering parameters. One-block and two-block formats differ. A depth word may be radial while an X allowance is diametral; the same letter can mean different things in different blocks. P and Q commonly identify the first and last numbered profile blocks. Check the referenced blocks, permitted profile direction changes, return path and compensation rules.

Why no universal cycle template is shown

The Haas G71 reference distinguishes path types and radial cut depth from diametral finish allowance. The Hurco ISNC G71 reference supplies its own two-block definition. A familiar cycle number does not establish compatible parameter meanings.

Grooving repeats plunges; threading synchronizes feed with rotation

A grooving or peck cycle divides a plunge into smaller advances and retracts. Some formats repeat the plunge at different Z positions to make a wider groove. G75 is one common outside/inside grooving family; face-grooving and peck-drilling families may use other codes. Check peck size, final depth, groove width and tool-width convention separately.

A G76-type threading cycle makes repeated synchronized passes with increasing depth. The thread lead determines the axial advance per spindle revolution. The cycle also needs control-specific information about final size, infeed, pass depth and runout. Use [[single-point-threading]] for pitch, lead and thread form; a threading cycle does not choose those dimensions for you.

Formats differ by control, so no complete parameter syntax is universal. See the Haas G75 reference and the G70/G76 entries in its lathe workbook as examples. [[cnc-turning-program]] spells out ordinary moves so the sequence can be read without decoding a cycle.
