A test indicator sweeps with a lever; a dial indicator measures plunger travel

InstrumentContact motionSetup use
Test indicatorA short stylus pivots through a small angle.Convenient for sweeping a vise jaw or a bore where access is tight.
Plunger dial indicatorThe spindle, or plunger, moves straight through a fixed mounting stem into the body.Useful for displacement checks and alignment when the plunger can face the measured motion.
Digital plunger indicator (shown below)A spindle/plunger still moves linearly; an LCD replaces the pointer and dial.Useful for the same kinds of displacement checks when its range, contact geometry and specifications fit the task.

These displays do not define the contact mechanism: the digital instrument shown here is still a plunger indicator. Test and plunger indicators can both compare alignment. Use the maker's stylus angle for a test indicator; on a common lever design the stylus lies nearly parallel to the measured surface. Align a plunger with the displacement being measured. Incorrect contact angles change the scale factor.

Keep the mounting rigid, preload within the instrument's range and confirm repeatability. During a [[milling-workholding|fixed-jaw sweep]], keep the spindle stopped and the contact geometry unchanged. For a bore-centering sweep, turn the spindle by hand using the approved setup method. Follow [[equipment-operating-procedure]] for the machine state during setup.

Contact angle affects the reading

Mitutoyo: Dial Test Indicators and the Cosine Effect shows the stylus geometry and correction. Its What is Cosine Error? video demonstrates the difference using a test indicator against a plunger indicator. Do not apply a plunger-indicator correction rule to a lever indicator without checking its instructions.

An edge finder locates the spindle center one tip radius from the edge

A mechanical edge finder has a spring-loaded contact tip that becomes concentric and then kicks sideways at contact. Use its specified spindle speed and approach method. Electronic edge finders and probes use different signals and procedures; do not assume they rotate like a mechanical edge finder.

Use a clean, defined edge, repeat the approach and account for the contact-tip radius. If the tip is 10 mm in diameter, the spindle center is 5 mm outside the edge at the trigger point. Setting that center position to zero without the radius correction moves every feature by 5 mm.

Example: the left face is X = 0 and positive X goes into the part to the right. At contact from the left with a 10 mm tip, the spindle center is X = −5 mm. Enter that position in the work-coordinate setting method for the control. On approach from a right-hand face, the sign changes. Sketch the spindle center and edge before choosing a sign.

Check both repeatability and the offset sign

Repeat the touch from the same direction. On manual feed screws, account for backlash when reversing; a handwheel reading can change while the table has not yet moved. Do not use a rough saw edge as though it were a finished datum. The radius correction is geometry, independent of the control's button sequence.

MIT's mill introduction covers mechanical edge finding and feed-screw backlash.

Home the machine, locate the part, then account for each tool's length

  • Machine home establishes the machine's reference positions. It does not locate the part.
  • Work zero is the origin chosen on the part or fixture. Its work offset relates that origin to the machine coordinates.
  • Tool length offset accounts for where a particular tool tip lies relative to the control's tool reference.

Choose the origin used by the drawing and program, locate X and Y, and establish Z using the approved touch-off or probing method. Account for any gauge or touch plate thickness. Confirm the active work offset, tool number and tool length before a trial path. Recheck after moving the part or changing how a tool is seated.
