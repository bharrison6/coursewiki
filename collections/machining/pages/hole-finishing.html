Choose the operation from the finished shape

Needed featureOperation
Improve the diameter and finish of a nearly correct holeReam
Enlarge an existing hole with control of its axis and diameterBore
Cylindrical recess with a flat shoulder for a fastener headCounterbore
Conical seat for a matching flat-head fastenerCountersink
Local flat bearing surface around a holeSpot face

Ream for size and finish; bore when the axis needs control

A reamer removes a small amount of material with multiple cutting edges. Prepare an undersize hole with enough material for the reamer to cut, rather than rub. Too much allowance overloads the tool; too little may leave the previous surface. Use the reamer maker's allowance for that diameter and material, not one stock allowance for every hole.

A reamer tends to follow the existing hole. Do not rely on it to relocate a badly misplaced or crooked hole. Boring enlarges an existing hole with a cutting edge whose path can be set relative to the machine axis. With a suitable setup and enough material left to clean up, it can correct location and straightness as well as diameter.

Use [[tolerances-and-fits]] to interpret the size limits and select inspection methods. See [[lathe-operations]] for boring and threading on the lathe.

A nominal tool size is not a finished-hole guarantee

Tool condition, alignment, runout, temperature and workholding affect the result. A reamer stamped with the desired diameter still needs an appropriate prepared hole and cutting conditions. Inspect the actual bore rather than accepting it from the tool marking.

Match the head seat to the fastener drawing

A counterbore has a cylindrical wall and a flat shoulder. A countersink has a conical seat. The screw head must match that seat's included angle; the thread diameter alone does not determine the head angle or the countersink diameter.

A spot face removes enough surface material to create a local flat seat for a head, nut or washer. It is useful on a rough or uneven surface. A spot face and a counterbore may use similar tools, but their required depth and purpose differ.

Read the specified diameters, depths and angle before cutting. A tool used only to break a sharp edge is not necessarily producing a fastener seat to size. Do not substitute a preferred cutter angle for the drawing requirement.

Test the choice before choosing the cutter

A smooth pin hole already on location suggests reaming. A hole whose axis must be corrected suggests boring, if there is enough material. A socket-head screw recessed into a cylindrical pocket suggests counterboring. A conical flat-head seat suggests countersinking.
