Start with what removes the material

A machine tool controls motion between a tool and the workpiece, the part being made. In material removal, the final shape is what remains after unwanted material is taken away.

CNC is a control method, not a removal family. Computer numerical control can guide a mill, lathe, grinder, EDM machine or cutting jet. See [[gcode-and-toolpaths]]. Forming moves material without removing it; see [[manufacturing-process-categories]] for the wider manufacturing map.

Conventional cutting uses defined cutting edges

  • [[lathe-operations|Turning]] rotates the work past a cutting tool; [[milling-operations|milling]] rotates the cutter and feeds it relative to the work.
  • [[drilling-operations|Drilling]] makes holes; [[cutoff-sawing|sawing]] separates stock, the starting material, with successive teeth.
  • Broaching uses a sequence of teeth to progressively cut a profile, such as a keyway. Dedicated tooling often suits repeated production, but broaching is not limited to large runs.
  • Shaping and planing use a back-and-forth straight cutting stroke. A shaper normally moves the tool; a planer normally moves the work.

A process name describes the action, not every possible shape. Milling can make curved contours as well as flat faces.

Abrasive processes use many small cutting grains

[[grinding-wheels|Grinding wheels]] hold abrasive grains that remove material. [[precision-grinding|Precision grinding]] controls size and surface texture, often on hardened parts. Grinding can also remove substantial stock; it is not restricted to light finishing.

Honing commonly uses abrasive stones with combined motions to refine a bore's size, form and surface. Lapping uses fine abrasive between a lap and the work to refine a surface. Neither is simply another name for grinding.

Nontraditional processes change the removal mechanism

  • Thermal: [[electrical-discharge-machining|EDM]], [[beam-and-jet-cutting|laser and plasma]] remove material with concentrated heat. EDM's energy supply is electrical, but its removal mechanism is thermal.
  • Electrochemical: [[electrochemical-machining|ECM]] uses electric current to dissolve metal.
  • Chemical: [[chemical-machining]] uses an etchant to dissolve exposed material.
  • Mechanical energy: [[beam-and-jet-cutting|waterjets]] and [[ultrasonic-machining]] erode material with moving water or abrasive particles.
