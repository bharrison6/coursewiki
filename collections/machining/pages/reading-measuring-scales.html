Read the unit and the graduation before the number

The same-looking line can mean 0.025 in, 0.5 mm, or something else. Identify the instrument and its scale before adding anything.

On a fractional rule, count spaces from the last whole inch using that edge's denominator. Seven 1/16 in spaces beyond 2 in gives 2 + 7/16 = 2.4375 in. That exact fraction-to-decimal conversion does not make the rule accurate to four decimal places.

On a decimal rule with 0.01 in spaces, eight spaces beyond 1.20 in gives 1.28 in. On a digital caliper, read the displayed unit and confirm whether the tool shows an absolute size or a deviation from a stored zero.

In inch shop speech, a thou is 0.001 in and a tenth often means 0.0001 in, one tenth of a thousandth. Write units and decimals to avoid confusing a shop tenth with 0.1 in.

An inch micrometer adds sleeve plus thimble

For the conventional 0.001 in micrometer, one turn advances the spindle 0.025 in. The sleeve has a line every 0.025 in; each fourth line marks another 0.100 in. The thimble divides one turn into 25 spaces of 0.001 in.

  1. Read the last sleeve line reached by the thimble edge. Count the 0.100 in intervals and the extra 0.025 in lines.
  2. Read the thimble line at the sleeve's horizontal datum line.
  3. Add the two. Include the range offset for a larger micrometer according to its markings.

Example: sleeve 0.300 in plus two 0.025 in lines, with thimble 18 at the datum:

0.300 + 0.050 + 0.018 = 0.368 in

Watch the rollover. Sleeve 0.375 and thimble 24 gives 0.399 in. The next 0.001 in step gives sleeve 0.400 and thimble 0: 0.400 in. Do not add the old 0.025 in interval again.

A vernier micrometer adds the fourth decimal place

The sleeve vernier divides a thimble space into ten parts. Read the sleeve and the whole 0.001 in thimble amount first, using the thimble graduation just passed when the datum falls between lines. Then find the sleeve vernier line that aligns with a thimble line.

Example: the last sleeve line is 0.450 in, the datum is between thimble 12 and 13, and sleeve vernier line 7 aligns:

0.4500 + 0.0120 + 7 × 0.0001 = 0.4627 in

The matching vernier line adds 0.0007 in, not 0.007 in. Do not round the thimble to 13 and then add the vernier. If the zero line aligns, add zero; the end line represents the same alignment at the next interval.

A metric micrometer includes the exposed half millimeter

On a conventional 0.01 mm micrometer, one turn moves the spindle 0.5 mm. The sleeve shows whole millimeters and half-millimeter marks; 50 thimble spaces divide the turn into 0.01 mm increments.

If 8 mm and the following 0.5 mm mark are exposed, and thimble 23 aligns, the reading is 8 + 0.5 + 0.23 = 8.73 mm. If that half-millimeter mark is still covered, the same thimble position gives 8.23 mm.

At the rollover, 8.49 mm is sleeve 8.0 plus thimble 49; 8.50 mm is sleeve 8.5 plus thimble 0. Use the marked unit; these increments are not the inch micrometer's increments converted by eye.

A 0.001 in vernier caliper adds the aligned division

For the 25-division inch vernier used here, each main-scale space is 0.025 in. Twenty-five vernier spaces occupy the length of 24 main-scale spaces, so each vernier space is 0.024 in. Their 0.001 in difference makes the fine reading possible.

  1. Read the last main-scale line at or before the vernier zero.
  2. Find the vernier division that lines up with a main-scale line.
  3. Add its division number × 0.001 in.

Vernier zero just beyond 1.325 in, with division 17 aligned, gives 1.325 + 0.017 = 1.342 in. It is the vernier zero, not the right end of the slider, that locates the coarse reading.

Other 0.001 in verniers can have different spacing

A 50-division vernier can span 49 main-scale spaces of 0.050 in. Each vernier space is 0.049 in, again leaving a 0.001 in difference. Read the actual main scale; do not apply the trainer's 0.025 in spacing to that tool. The Starrett catalog, page 102, illustrates the 50-division arrangement.

A dial caliper adds the beam reading and the dial remainder

On an inch dial caliper marked 0.100 in per revolution, the dial's 100 divisions each represent 0.001 in. Read whole inches and completed 0.100 in intervals on the beam, then add the dial reading.

Beam 1.200 in and dial 47 gives 1.200 + 0.047 = 1.247 in. Beam 1.200 plus dial 99 is 1.299 in. At the next thousandth the beam reaches 1.300 and the dial returns to zero.

Some dial calipers use a different travel per revolution. Check the dial markings rather than assuming this example applies. Clean the jaws, close gently, check zero, and confirm the reading again after measuring.
