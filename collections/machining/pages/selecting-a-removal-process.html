Screen the material and feature before choosing a machine

  • Identify the material, its condition and conductivity. Check [[material-families]] and [[material-properties]].
  • Mark the feature: through profile, blind cavity, shallow recess or finished surface. Identify tool access and any start-hole requirement.
  • Record thickness, minimum internal radius and the thinnest remaining feature.
  • Read the drawing's dimensional and surface requirements. Decide whether a thermal HAZ or recast layer is acceptable.
  • Shortlist processes, then check actual equipment capability, finishing needs and production quantity.

Hardness alone does not select a process. A hard nonconductive ceramic rules out ordinary EDM even though EDM can cut hardened steel. See [[tolerances-and-fits]] and [[surface-texture]] for what the finished part must meet.

Keep conventional machining on the candidate list

For an accessible flat face, hole or round shaft in a machinable material, conventional cutting may be the simplest route. Use the [[material-removal-processes|family map]] to reach those methods. A part may combine sawing, milling and grinding, or use waterjet for a blank followed by machining of critical faces.

Increasing thickness can increase cutting time, taper or flushing difficulty. A thin remaining wall may deflect or be damaged even when cutting forces are small. Ask for evidence of capability on the actual material and geometry rather than relying on a universal tolerance ranking.
