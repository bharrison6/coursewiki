The spindle connection and the tool grip are separate choices

A holder has a machine end and a cutting-tool end. “ER” describes a collet system at the tool end; it does not tell you which spindle the holder fits.

InterfaceHow to recognize the idea
R8A taper seats in the spindle and a drawbar threads into the collet or holder to pull it in. A direct R8 collet grips a matching tool shank.
CAT / BTSteep-taper holder families with flanges and retention knobs, also called pull studs, for compatible spindle retention and tool changing. Similar-looking tapers do not establish interchangeability: family, size, flange and retention knob must match the machine.
TTSThe Tormach Tooling System uses a straight holder shank gripped in a compatible collet and a shoulder that seats against the spindle face. TTS is a toolholding system, not a CAT/BT-style taper. Its seated shoulder provides a repeatable axial reference.

Identify the installed interface before selecting a holder. A Tormach name alone does not identify the spindle system or drawbar arrangement.

Public toolholder references

Haas: Tool Holder Essentials compares holder choices. Tormach's R8/TTS explanation is for the machine configuration named in that manual, not a statement of this shop's inventory.

Choose a collet, end mill holder or drill chuck for the grip needed

  • Collet: a split sleeve closes around the tool shank. Match the series and the specified gripping diameter or range. A direct R8 collet and an ER collet are different systems.
  • Set-screw end mill holder: a fitted bore locates the shank and a screw bears on the tool's designated flat. Match the bore to the shank and the flat to the screw; follow the holder's seating and tightening method.
  • Drill chuck: adjustable jaws grip round-shank drilling tools. An ordinary drill chuck is not an end-milling holder; its grip and mounting are not intended for milling side loads.

Grip the intended shank surface, not the cutting flutes. Keep the tool's specified engagement in the holder while leaving only the reach needed to cut and clear the part.

Snap the ER collet into its nut before threading the nut onto the holder

  1. Match the ER series, nut, collet and tool shank. Read the collet's actual range; the ER series number is not the tool diameter. Do not force an oversized shank into it or collapse it below its rated range.
  2. Clean and inspect the holder socket, collet, nut and tool shank. Follow the manufacturer's lubrication instructions for the named surfaces; do not treat every taper and gripping surface alike.
  3. Engage the collet's extraction groove with the nut's eccentric retaining ring. Tilt and snap it into place as instructed by the maker.
  4. Thread the nut-and-collet assembly loosely onto the holder.
  5. Insert the tool with the required shank engagement and planned stickout. Tighten to the specified torque using the proper wrench and holding fixture.

Range example: a collet explicitly rated 9–10 mm can grip a 9.5 mm shank; it cannot grip a 10.2 mm shank. This is a reading exercise, not a universal collapse range for every ER collet.

Why the snap matters

The eccentric ring retains and extracts the collet as the nut is loosened. Missing that engagement can prevent correct seating. Use REGO-FIX's ER assembly instructions for the mechanism and Haas: ER Collet Essentials for a public demonstration.

Runout changes the chip taken by each tooth; stickout changes stiffness

Runout is the variation seen as a tool or reference shank rotates about the spindle axis. If one cutting edge projects farther than the others, it can carry more of the load. Dirt, damaged seating surfaces, a poor collet fit, the tool, holder or spindle can contribute. Clean and reseat before blaming cutting data; measure against the job and tool requirements.

Stickout is the unsupported reach from the grip. Keep it as short as the feature and holder clearance allow. Do not solve a reach problem by leaving too little shank in the collet. Explore the effect in [[tool-deflection]]; use [[milling-chatter]] when vibration appears during a cut.
