Saw a blank with room for finishing

Sawing often turns a long bar or a large plate into a manageable blank: the starting piece for machining. Plan both the material left for finishing and the kerf, the slot removed by the saw. Kerf is material lost between pieces; it is not finishing allowance.

Choose allowance from the required finished dimensions, cut quality, and later operations. A saw cut is not automatically a finished datum surface. Check dimensions and squareness before using it to locate the next cut. See [[measurement-tools]] and [[layout-and-angle-measurement]].

Match the saw's motion to the cut

SawHow it removes materialTypical use
Power hacksawA straight blade moves back and forth, cutting on one stroke and relieving on the other. Cutting-stroke direction depends on the machine.Occasional straight cutoff work; the return stroke does no cutting.
Horizontal bandsawA continuous toothed loop advances through material clamped in a vise.Bar, tube, and structural sections cut to length. Miter capability depends on the head or vise design.
Vertical bandsawThe band passes through a table while the work is fed along a path.Straight cuts and curved outlines in plate; see [[vertical-band-machining]].
Cold circular sawA toothed circular blade shears chips.Repeatable cutoffs where squareness and surface finish matter. Inspect for burrs even when the cut looks clean.
Abrasive cutoff sawA thin abrasive wheel grinds through the section.Cutoff work suited to the wheel's material rating, including some hardened materials. Heat and cut quality can require further finishing.

“Cold” distinguishes chip cutting from abrasive or friction cutting; it does not mean zero heat. Use [[saw-blade-selection]] for toothed blades. An abrasive wheel has its own selection rules.

Hold every piece, including pieces in a bundle

NEVER hand-hold material for a horizontal saw cut. Use the machine's vise or a suitable fixture. Support long material at the saw's working height so it does not tilt the clamped piece. Arrange support for the cutoff without trapping the blade as the cut closes.

Nesting means arranging several pieces for one cut. Clamping the outside of a bundle does not prove that its inner pieces are held. Use a nesting arrangement and clamping system approved for the machine, and select the blade for the resulting section.

  • Identify the material, section shape, finished size, and finishing allowance.
  • Confirm that the vise or fixture grips each piece and that long stock is supported.
  • Account for the kerf when locating the cut or setting a length stop.
  • Plan how the cutoff will be supported as the blade breaks through.
  • After cutting, inspect the blank and remove the burr before the next setup.

At break-through, the last connection between blank and stock disappears. Cutting resistance changes, and a thin lip of displaced metal may remain at the edge: a burr. See [[bench-work]] for deburring, and [[machine-hazards]], [[ppe]], and [[metal-chips]] for hazards and precautions.

Choose speed, feed, and coolant together

Band speed is the distance the blade travels per minute, usually stated in feet/min or m/min. Feed is the rate or force that advances the blade into the work. They are different adjustments. Material grade and hardness, blade material, tooth geometry, and section shape all affect the starting settings.

Use the blade maker's cutting chart for the actual material, then follow the machine manual for setting it. Follow the blade maker's break-in procedure for a new blade. There is no single speed table or fixed percentage adjustment that fits every saw. Connect these terms to [[feeds-and-speeds]].

On a saw designed to use cutting fluid, the fluid helps lubricate the cut, carry away heat, and clear chips. Use the approved fluid, concentration, and delivery method. Some abrasive cutoff systems are dry and others are wet; do not add coolant to a dry-only setup.

Why finer teeth do not fix every bad cut

More teeth share the load, but smaller gullets have less room for chips. Too much feed can overload the teeth; too little can make them rub. A wandering cut also calls for checking the blade condition, tension, guides, and workholding against the manuals. Changing speed alone cannot correct loose work or a worn guide.
