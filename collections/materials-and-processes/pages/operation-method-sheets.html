A method sheet tells another person how to make and check the part

A drawing describes the required part. An operation method sheet describes how to make it. A process plan or routing sheet serves the same planning purpose, although the amount of detail varies. Write one row for each operation, in the order it will be performed.

Start with the part number and name, drawing revision, date, material and raw stock size: the starting piece before machining or cutting. Use actual dimensions and units. A material family alone is not a purchasing specification; identify the material needed for this part.

What belongs in each operation row
FieldWhat the next person needs
OperationAn action and its intended result, such as drill two through holes.
Equipment and setup/toolingThe equipment, cutting tool, support, holding method and reference face or edge.
Process parametersSettings with units, or an identified approved setup instruction. Do not write an unexplained number for speed or feed.
Setup and cycle timeTime to prepare the operation, separate from time to repeat it on one part.
Pass/fail criteria and inspection toolsThe required result, permitted limits and how to measure or compare it.

Keep quality checkpoints, failed-part decisions, actual times and improvement notes with the sheet. Equipment permission and operating procedures still come from [[equipment-operating-procedure]]; a method sheet is not machine training.

Establish reference surfaces before locating features

Choose the order around what each later step needs. Make a straight, square reference edge before measuring hole locations from it. Make features while there is enough material to support and hold the work. Inspect a feature before the next step hides it.

Holes may be easier to make before small parts are cut free from a larger piece. Final finishing usually follows cutting and drilling, so those operations do not damage the finished surface. Inspect afterward too: finishing can change dimensions.

Why there is no universal operation order

A feature may become inaccessible after assembly; a coating may need to be applied first. A larger blank may help holding, but its final cut must preserve the reference used for the holes. Record the reason for a departure from the usual sequence. The five [[manufacturing-process-categories|process categories]] describe kinds of change, not a fixed order.

Replace “make it right” with an action and an acceptance test

“Drill the holes neatly” leaves the next person guessing. “Drill two through holes at the drawing coordinates; check diameter and location against revision A” gives an action and a result, but still needs the equipment, support, tooling and settings in the row.

Pass means every stated criterion is met. Fail means identify and hold the part for a decision; do not quietly send it to the next operation. Rework is a specified additional operation that can bring the part into conformance. Removing more material cannot restore an undersize outside dimension or shrink an oversize hole.

  • Match the part, drawing and method-sheet revisions.
  • Check that every operation names equipment, tooling, holding and references.
  • Fill required settings with units before releasing the plan.
  • Give every quality checkpoint a measurable limit or an explicit comparison and an inspection method.
  • Define who decides rework or rejection when a check fails.

Record what changed so the next batch can repeat the improvement

Record setup time once per setup and cycle time per piece. State when each timer starts and stops; include inspection consistently. Keep quantity attempted, quantity accepted, scrap count and rework count. A reworked part may later pass, so these counts are not all separate groups to add together.

For each trial, record material identity, tool, setting and units, holding arrangement, actual measurements and the problem observed. A useful photo shows the reference edge, stop or tool position; caption it with the operation and revision. Use photos you have permission to share, with no personal information.
