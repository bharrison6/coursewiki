Powder bed fusion repeats a spread, select, and lower cycle

Powder bed fusion (PBF) is one additive-manufacturing family. A machine spreads a thin powder layer, applies thermal energy to selected regions, lowers or advances the build, and repeats. Unselected powder remains around the growing parts until they are unpacked.

The family name does not specify one material or energy source. Polymer and metal examples can share the layer cycle while differing in temperature, atmosphere, thermal behavior, support strategy, cooldown, powder recovery, postprocessing and inspection.

Start with [[additive-manufacturing-process-families]] for the seven-family map. PBF thermally fuses powder during the build; it is not binder jetting, where deposited binder first creates a green shape.

Readable process sources

The NIST PBF program documents metal laser powder bed fusion work. The polymer examples below use the public Formlabs SLS process description and HP's mechanism comparison. Vendor sources establish their stated systems, not universal rankings or machine settings.

Polymer SLS and MJF select powder in different ways

Selective laser sintering (SLS)
A laser scans selected regions of a polymer powder layer and thermally joins the particles. Unfused powder surrounds the parts in the stated process, so dedicated support structures are generally unnecessary; the build must cool before unpacking and depowdering.
Multi Jet Fusion (MJF)
A printhead places fusing and detailing agents on polymer powder, then applied heat fuses selected regions. The agents control fusion; they are not binder that leaves a green part for later sintering.

Both examples belong to PBF because powder is thermally fused during the build. Neither description proves that any polymer powder, color, wall, gap or enclosed cavity will work on a particular machine.

Loose powder can carry surrounding geometry during these stated polymer builds, but it must still be removed. A sealed hollow region can trap powder. An internal passage needs an accessible route for cleaning and inspection; “no dedicated supports” does not mean “no design constraints.”

Metal laser and electron-beam PBF need process-specific thermal reasoning

The metal examples here use a laser or electron beam to melt or fuse selected regions of metal powder. The part develops through repeated melt-and-solidify events, so orientation and connection to the build system can affect heat flow, residual stress, distortion and later removal.

In a stated laser PBF case, sacrificial supports may anchor an overhang to the build plate and conduct heat away. Loose metal powder surrounding the part is not automatically an adequate thermal path. That does not create a universal rule that every metal PBF system and geometry needs the same supports.

Electron-beam PBF has process-specific strategies and exceptions. Current commercial examples can reduce or replace traditional supports for some parts. Record the exact machine, material, melt strategy, orientation and acceptance plan instead of transferring one support rule to every metal powder process.

Why the support statement is qualified

Ohio State's laser-PBF research summary explains support structures used to anchor overhangs and address thermally induced distortion in that context. A current Colibrium EB-PBF example documents process strategies that reduce or eliminate traditional supports for some applications. The exception is why this page states a case rather than an absolute.

Design powder access and the post-build route with the part

  • Mark enclosed volumes, channels and lattice spaces; give loose powder a stated removal and inspection route.
  • State the material, machine/process variant, energy route and build orientation rather than writing only “PBF.”
  • Identify what supports geometry and, for the stated thermal case, what anchors the part and conducts heat.
  • Plan cooldown, unpacking, depowdering and any support removal before choosing inaccessible geometry.
  • List required postprocessing and inspection. A completed build is not automatically a qualified service part.

Material properties and acceptance depend on the qualified material-process-part route. Use [[metals-and-their-properties]] and [[plastics-as-materials]] for material foundations; use [[heat-treating-metals]] only when a specified metal route actually calls for that operation.
