Digital uses two states; analog uses a range

A digital input interprets two states: LOW/HIGH, or 0/1. The wire still carries a physical voltage; the receiving circuit decides which logic state it represents. A voltage in the undefined region between valid LOW and HIGH levels is not a useful third state.

An analog signal represents a quantity by a continuous range of values. A potentiometer wired between ground and 5 V can provide a changing voltage at its middle terminal. On a classic Uno R3, the usual analog measurement range is nominally 0–5 V with the default reference. That is not a universal sensor range.

Phototransistor with a load resistorAnalog response to light unless a threshold circuit converts it to digital.
Pressure or proximity sensorDepends on the model: analog voltage, switching output, or digital data. Read its documentation.

A bare resistive sensor does not generate a usable voltage by itself: its surrounding circuit matters. A module may offer both analog and thresholded digital outputs.

Phototransistor circuits can measure light or switch at a threshold

Vishay's Ambient Light Sensors — Circuit and Window Design shows both light-meter circuits and light-switch circuits. The phototransistor current changes with illumination; the surrounding circuit determines whether the output preserves a range or switches between logic states.

Why an ultrasonic measurement can be digital

The HC-SR04 manufacturer datasheet, hosted by SparkFun describes an Echo pulse whose width represents the range. Digital describes the two voltage states; the information can be carried by their timing. A single digitalRead tells you the current state, not the pulse duration.

The ADC reads voltage; PWM controls time

[[analog-to-digital-conversion]] turns an analog input into a number the program can use. [[pulse-width-modulation]] switches a digital output on and off for a controlled fraction of each cycle. Neither operation changes the fact that the processor stores and works with digital data.
