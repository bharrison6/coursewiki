A generator converts motion into electrical energy

A generator does not create energy. It takes mechanical work at a shaft and delivers electrical energy to a circuit, with some energy becoming heat along the way.

A turbine is a rotating machine driven by a moving fluid. Steam, flowing water, wind, or hot combustion gases can turn its blades. The turbine turns the generator; the generator supplies electricity. An engine can also drive a generator without a turbine.

[[img:electrical-systems/generation-chain.svg|Heat makes steam; steam turns a turbine; the turbine turns a generator; the generator supplies electricity. Water, wind and combustion gases can drive turbines too.]]

How rotation produces electricity

Relative motion between a magnetic field and conductors induces a voltage. With a closed circuit, current flows and the shaft must supply work against the electrical load. Motor action and generator action reverse the direction of energy conversion; see [[electric-motor-principles]]. Actual machines and controls determine how they can operate in either direction.

See [[power-and-efficiency]] for losses and [[ac-and-dc]] for the electrical output forms.

Different heat sources can feed the same steam cycle

Burning fossil fuels or biomass can heat water to make steam. Nuclear fission supplies heat without combustion. Geothermal systems use heat from the Earth, and concentrated solar systems use mirrors to collect sunlight as heat. The equipment differs, but a steam-based plant follows the same basic chain: heat, moving steam, shaft rotation, electricity.

Not every turbine uses steam. A hydroelectric turbine takes energy from water flowing downhill. A wind turbine takes energy from moving air. A combustion turbine uses hot gases directly; a combined-cycle plant also recovers exhaust heat for a steam cycle.

Photovoltaic cells skip the turbine

A photovoltaic (PV) cell converts light directly into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect in a semiconductor. A connected load receives DC electricity. An inverter can convert that DC to AC.

Solar PV and concentrated solar thermal are different routes. PV produces electricity in the cell; solar thermal first collects heat. That distinction matters when considering [[energy-storage-methods|thermal storage]].

A generation mix is more than a list of percentages

A generation mix describes how much electricity comes from each source over a stated place and time. Installed capacity describes how much power equipment is rated to supply. Those are different measures: a large rated capacity does not mean a source supplied the same share of annual energy.

Predict: which generator produces more energy?

Compare two hypothetical generators over the same 365-day, 8760-hour period. Generator A is rated at 1 MW with a given 25% capacity factor. Generator B is rated at 0.5 MW with a given 60% capacity factor. Which produces more energy over the year? Decide before opening the check.

Check the rated-power and period-energy comparison

Period energy = rated power × period hours × capacity factor
Capacity factor = period energy ÷ (rated power × period hours)

Generator B has the smaller rating but produces more energy in this given example. MW measures power; MWh measures energy over time. Do not treat 1 MW as 1 MWh for the year, and write 25% as 0.25 in the multiplication, not 25.

Capacity factor is not conversion efficiency, the percentage of hours switched on, dispatchability, reliability, or a source's share of an energy mix. Partial-load operation contributes too; the factor does not require a generator to alternate only between off and full power.

These ratings and factors are fixed hypothetical inputs for the stated period, not real-plant data. EIA's published factors use time-adjusted capacity methods; this example does not reproduce that method. The EIA capacity and generation FAQ distinguishes rated power from energy produced over a period and defines the capacity-factor comparison.

Compare cost, emissions, variability, and dispatchability — the ability to adjust output when requested. Wind and sunlight vary with weather and time. Fuelled generation and reservoir hydro can often be scheduled, but fuel, water, maintenance and operating limits still matter. Storage shifts energy in time; it is not an extra primary energy source.
