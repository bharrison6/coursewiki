Choose a graphic for the question you need to answer

Engineering graphics communicate technical ideas and problem solutions. Aim for accuracy, simplicity, and clarity. A graphic succeeds when another person can use it for the intended task.

Ideation sketch
A quick drawing for exploring and comparing ideas. Designers and teammates use it to discuss shape, motion, or arrangement before committing to detailed geometry. Label uncertain sizes and assumptions.
CAD model
A digital description of geometry. Designers use it to develop parts, examine how components fit, and support analysis or manufacturing preparation. A realistic appearance alone does not prove that the design works.
Engineering drawing
Selected views with dimensions, tolerances, and notes. People making, assembling, and inspecting the product use this information to understand what is required. See [[working-drawings-and-title-blocks]].

Use testing to send the design back for refinement

Begin with the task and its requirements: what must the design do, and what limits must it satisfy? Sketch alternatives, develop promising geometry, then test it against those requirements. Testing may involve calculations, simulation, a prototype, or a combination.

[[img:engineering-graphics/drawings-in-design/design-loop.svg|Design sequence: define the need, sketch alternatives, develop a CAD model and draft drawings, test, refine, then check and release. A return arrow sends refinement back to the model and drawings.]]

When a test reveals a problem, revise the design and test again. Update the affected graphics so that the model, drawings, and test record describe the same design.

Why the process is a loop

A model can reveal a space conflict that was hard to see in a sketch. A prototype can reveal a behavior that the model did not predict. These findings can send the team back to the geometry or even to the original concept. Sketches remain useful during refinement; drawings can be drafted before the design is final.

Release identifies the information approved for use

Release means approving a defined design state for its intended use through the organization's review process. Before release, check the model, drawing set, and supporting requirements together. A saved file is not automatically an approved file.

A maker needs part requirements; an assembler needs component relationships; an inspector needs criteria for deciding whether the result is acceptable. Match the information to the reader's job. For drawing-based work, use [[creating-drawings-from-cad-models]] to prepare the sheets and [[cad-history-and-sharing]] to distinguish an editable design from a preserved milestone.

Does every design require a separate drawing?

Some workflows communicate requirements through an annotated model and associated data. Others use drawings as the controlling documents. Follow the agreed deliverables and identify which information governs. A plain shaded model does not automatically communicate material, tolerances, or approval status.

Follow the specified drawing standard and its applicable edition

Standards give drawing readers shared rules for interpreting views, lines, dimensions, and symbols. The ASME Y14 series is widely used in US engineering drawing practice; ISO technical drawing standards are widely used internationally. These are not exclusive geographic boundaries. Read the drawing's stated requirements rather than choosing a convention from a country name.

A convention is an accepted way of presenting information. A company may add conventions for sheet layout or numbering, but those choices must remain consistent with the governing requirements. Use the current applicable edition specified for the work; do not silently apply an old classroom example or change an existing drawing's governing edition.

Check units, projection method, and the stated standards before interpreting a drawing. Continue with [[orthographic-projection]] and [[choosing-drawing-views]] for view interpretation. This overview does not reproduce standard clauses or serve as a compliance checklist.
