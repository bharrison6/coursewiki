Use a certification to document assessed skills, not to replace design experience

A CAD certification is a provider's record that you passed a defined assessment. Depending on the program, that assessment may cover drawing interpretation, sketches, parts, assemblies, documentation, collaboration, manufacturing, or analysis. It can give a learner a concrete practice goal and give an employer one more piece of evidence about a specific product or workflow.

Its meaning is bounded. A credential does not guarantee employment, prove that every model is manufacturable, or replace a portfolio, coursework, supervised practice, and engineering judgment. Product names and versions also matter: proficiency in one CAD system does not automatically transfer to every other system.

This overview leads with mechanical CAD. Architecture and construction, CAM, simulation, additive manufacturing, product-data management, and platform roles are useful branches when they match the work you want to do. Program information below was checked against official sources on October 29, 2026; recheck the linked catalog before registering.

Choose the product and work first, then the credential level

Do not begin by looking for a universal Associate → Professional → Expert ladder. Providers use those words differently, and some products offer only selected levels. Begin with the software and tasks used in your course, internship, workplace, or intended field.

Core mechanical design
Look for sketching, parametric part modeling, assemblies, drawings, dimensions, materials, and model checks.
Specialized design
Sheet metal, surfacing, weldments, mold tools, drawing tools, and generative design usually require narrower practice than a general design credential.
Manufacturing and additive
CAM, turning, milling, additive-process, and design-for-manufacturing credentials connect geometry to process planning. They are not substitutes for machine or shop authorization.
Simulation
Static, thermal, flow, frequency, nonlinear, and other analysis credentials assess both software operation and interpretation. Passing does not make an unchecked result physically valid.
Platform and data roles
Cloud collaboration, revisions, lifecycle, PDM, and administrator credentials may assess data workflows rather than only geometry creation.

Read the assessed skills on the exact credential page. A badge for completing a course, a training certificate, and a certification earned by passing an assessment are not automatically the same thing.

Compare SOLIDWORKS and Autodesk mechanical paths

SOLIDWORKS

The official SOLIDWORKS catalog groups the general design path as Design Associate, Design Professional, and Design Expert, but the levels assess different depth rather than the same checklist three times.

Design Associate (CSWA)
Foundational sketches, common part features, patterns, dimensions, materials and mass properties, component insertion and mates, drawing views, and annotations.
Design Professional (CSWP)
Professional parametric part design from drawings, linked dimensions and equations, configurations and design tables, mass-property changes, and assembly modeling across three segments.
Design Expert (CSWE)
Advanced part and assembly problem solving, including multibody/imported geometry, complex features, in-context work, configurations, and advanced bills of materials. Eligibility requires CSWP plus at least four of exactly these five advanced-topic exams: Sheet Metal, Weldments, Surfacing, Mold Tools/Mold Making, and Drawing Tools.

Those five design specialties form the CSWE prerequisite set. Other Professional credentials—such as CAM, MBD, API, and Flow Simulation—assess valuable but different work and are not listed as substitutes in the CSWE requirement. Additive Manufacturing Associate assesses processes, materials, design, slicing, and preparation without requiring SOLIDWORKS software during the assessment.

The simulation family is separate: Simulation Associate covers statics, materials, loads, mesh, and result checks; Simulation Professional adds static, frequency, thermal, beam/shell, and convergence work; Simulation Expert requires CSWP, CSWA-S, and CSWP-S and adds advanced contact, composites, thermal, dynamics, and nonlinear analysis. Flow Simulation Professional is another specialty, not a step in that three-credential prerequisite chain.

Cloud xDesign and xShape credentials are not alternate names for desktop CSWA/CSWP. The xDesign Professional and xShape Professional pages require the relevant app, prescribed training, 3DSwymer Associate, ENOVIA Collaborative Industry Innovator Associate, and a new collaboration space. Exact student-cloud bundle mapping remains unclear, so confirm access before choosing one.

Route matters for students. Through the Academic Certification program, free exams depend on the school being an Academic Certification Provider, an active subscription, and region-specific license minimums; this is not an entitlement for every student. The current Design Premium for Students offer separately lists CSWA and CSWP attempts, while free Design Standard software does not list them. Recheck the current offer before purchase. Ordinary CSWP publishes no general prior-certification requirement, except that K–12 students using an Academic Certification Provider must first pass CSWA. The distinct CSWP-Academic route requires CSWA-Academic at least 30 days earlier and qualified, proctored institutional delivery.

Autodesk

Autodesk names four broad levels: Certified User (ACU), Certified Associate (ACA), Certified Professional (ACP), and Certified Expert (ACE). Not every product has every level, and the official overview does not establish a universal prerequisite ladder.

Mechanical starting points include ACU exams for AutoCAD, Inventor, and Fusion; ACA in CAD for Mechanical Design; ACP in Inventor for Mechanical Design, AutoCAD for Design and Drafting, and Design for Manufacturing; and ACE in Generative Design for Manufacturing. Optional extensions include Fusion CAM credentials, ACP in Simulation for Static Stress Analysis, and AEC credentials for Revit, Civil 3D, and BIM management.

ACU registration runs through Certiport; Autodesk's current ACU guidance says exams may be delivered online or in person, so do not assume a testing-center-only route. ACA, ACP, and ACE pages instead route candidates through Autodesk's application/dashboard to Pearson VUE or OnVUE. Those ACA/ACP/ACE exams use selected-response questions rather than live CAD software, so preparation should include concepts and workflow decisions as well as software practice. See Autodesk certification support for current policies and preparation routes. Free Autodesk education software and free learning resources do not establish a free exam or student discount.

Compare Onshape, Creo, NX, and Solid Edge

Onshape
The live Learning Center certification catalog lists Certified Onshape Associate for parts, multipart design, assemblies, drawings, and collaboration, and Certified Onshape Professional for advanced modeling, motion, in-context work, versioning, branching, and linked-data collaboration. No Associate prerequisite is published for Professional. Register through the Learning Center and its Training Dashboard. A student Associate voucher has been described only through a conditional preparation route; current course access and voucher availability must be confirmed.
PTC Creo
The current PTC Learning Connector uses Product Proficiency for foundational sketches, features, assemblies, properties, and analysis; Modeling Specialist for advanced design intent, sketches, and solids; and Surfacing, Sheetmetal, Assembly, and Documentation specialists for their named workflows. Older User/Associate/Professional labels should not replace the live catalog. Enroll through the specific Learning Connector exam with appropriate active LEARN access; learning paths are recommended, and the current FAQ says prior certifications are not prerequisites. A separate academic certification-only route offers Product Proficiency, Modeling Specialist, Assembly Specialist, and Documentation Specialist after Proxi.id academic verification. It does not include every specialist or prove a universal student entitlement.
Siemens NX
The live Siemens Xcelerator Academy catalog lists NX Design Associate for solid and assembly models, modeling strategies, parametric/non-parametric methods, and interpart links; NX Design Professional adds curves and surfaces, PMI, validation, downstream model use, and drafting. The NX Design learning map makes Associate a prerequisite for Professional. Register from the catalog's Buy route. Optional NX CAM Associate/Professional and Additive Manufacturing Associate paths assess manufacturing work. Academic discounts or free vouchers may depend on institutional licensing; no universal student entitlement is established.
Siemens Solid Edge
The Solid Edge registry publishes Associate, Professional, and Expert. Associate covers introductory parts, assemblies, sketches, and drawings; Professional adds advanced synchronous, sheet-metal, assembly, and documentation work. Associate before Professional is recommended, not stated as mandatory; Expert requires Professional and assesses advanced ordered/synchronous modeling and detailing. Use Getting Started to register, complete the profile, review the guide, and select an exam. Enrollment pages currently display the exams as free, but verify region and delivered software version.

Keep CATIA V5 and 3DEXPERIENCE credentials separate

Dassault Systèmes publishes two different families. 3DEXPERIENCE role certifications include Associate roles such as Mechanical Designer, Mechanical & Shape Designer, SheetMetal Designer, and Function Driven Generative Designer. Associate exams use the Edu Space and certification-credit route with access to the appropriate roles/apps. The Professional registry covers advanced Mechanical Designer, Function Driven Generative Designer, and SheetMetal Designer work through authorized certification centers—public centers for general candidates or private academic centers for their students. Role trigrams identify software roles, not separate marketed exam codes.

CATIA V5 certification is another center-delivered family, marketed at Associate and Expert levels for Part Design, Assembly Design, and Surface Design. Its catalog describes training and regular practice as preparation, but the inspected pages do not publish a prior Associate credential as a universal Expert gate. Contact an authorized center for the available V5 route rather than registering through the 3DEXPERIENCE Associate Edu Space.

Student CATIA/3DEXPERIENCE offers can bundle software and exams under eligibility, billing-country, age, and noncommercial-use conditions. Official pages conflict on the exact bundled exam roster and renewal wording, so confirm the cart and current student terms rather than promising a particular set of credentials.

Separate prerequisites, preparation, access, and payment

  • Credential prerequisite: a prior certification the exact exam page says you must hold. Examples include the published CSWE, SOLIDWORKS Simulation Expert, NX Design Professional, and Solid Edge Expert chains.
  • Recommended preparation: experience hours, courses, learning paths, or practice described as readiness guidance. Do not silently convert a recommendation into an admission rule.
  • Software or platform access: the app, version, role, testing client, collaboration space, or live software needed to prepare for or take that route.
  • Registration condition: account, identity, age/consent, testing-center, remote-proctor, institutional, region, or academic-verification requirements.
  • Payment condition: a public purchase, institutional voucher, subscription benefit, bundle, or conditional student offer. Free student software is not the same as a free exam.

Voucher expiration and credential validity are also different. For example, Certiport governs ACU credential policy while Autodesk's other credentials use Autodesk/Pearson routes. Confirm the exact program, country, tax, proctoring, retake, voucher, and validity terms immediately before paying or scheduling.

Use one verified route from comparison through preparation

  1. Name the product and work you want to demonstrate: general mechanical design, drawings, manufacturing, simulation, or data workflow.
  2. Open the provider's current catalog and the exact credential page. Record the title, assessed skills, delivery route, and published prerequisites.
  3. Mark recommendations separately from requirements. Confirm that you can access the needed software, account, testing center, or remote-proctor setup.
  4. Ask your institution about participation or vouchers without assuming it has them. Verify any student offer, region, expiration, and checkout total directly.
  5. Use official preparation materials without copying exam questions, answer keys, or protected sample parts. Practise the underlying workflow with original drawings and models.
