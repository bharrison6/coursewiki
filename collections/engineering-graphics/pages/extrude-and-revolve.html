Extrude carries a cross-section in a straight direction

For a plain solid extrusion, select a closed sketch region and give it depth. A rectangular region makes a block; a circular region makes a cylinder. Without draft, the cross-section remains constant along the extrusion.

A boss/base extrusion creates or adds solid material. An extruded cut removes material where the extrusion intersects the target solid. Check the arrow, preview, and selected bodies. A cut pointing away from the part cannot remove material from it.

In Onshape, choose the result operation New, Add, or Remove. In SOLIDWORKS, check the boss/cut command and merge or feature scope settings. Adding touching material and creating a separate body are different intentions.

Closed profiles, thin features, and surfaces

Closed regions are the simplest starting point for solids. Open sketches can be used with thin-feature or surface options, but those are different constructions: a surface has no solid volume, while a thin feature adds a wall thickness. Check the result type before accepting the preview. See Onshape: Extrude.

Choose the end condition for what should happen after an edit

ConditionWhat controls the endUse when
BlindA specified distance in the selected direction.A pocket must keep a particular depth.
Through allAll target material along the selected direction.A cut must remain through as the part thickens. Check body scope and one- versus two-direction settings.
Up to nextThe next terminating surface that intercepts the entire profile.The next suitable boundary is the intended stop. It is not simply the first edge encountered.
Up to surface / faceA selected terminating surface or face.The feature must follow that particular boundary when it moves. The selection becomes a dependency.
Mid-planeA total depth split equally about the sketch plane.The part must stay centered. In SOLIDWORKS, a 14 mm Mid Plane depth extends 7 mm each way.

Worked thickness edit. One rectangular cut starts on the top face of one flat plate, travels downward normal to that face, and removes material from that selected body only. Compare a distance-controlled blind cut with a through-all cut before and after the plate becomes thicker.

All three sections use the same vertical scale: 8 drawing units per millimeter.

Design goalChoice that survives the editWhy
Opening must stay throughThrough allThe termination follows the selected plate thickness.
Pocket must retain a floorBlind, with a depth smaller than the final thicknessThe fixed distance intentionally stops before the far face.

This teaching model does not cover up-to-face, up-to-next, multiple bodies, opposite directions or revolved cuts. Check the selected sketch region, direction and body scope in the CAD preview.

Do not substitute an oversized blind cut for a through-all requirement. It may work today and leave a thin floor after a later edit. Conversely, through all is wrong for a pocket that must retain its floor.

Onshape uses a Symmetric option for supported end types. Dialog names and available conditions depend on the selected operation; inspect the preview and measured result rather than transferring every setting by name.

Revolve rotates a section around an axis

Select a section, an axis of rotation, and an angle. A full turn is 360°; a smaller angle produces a partial rotational shape. A boss revolve adds material, and a revolved cut removes it. Start with a closed section on one side of the axis for a straightforward solid revolve.

A straight sketch line, centerline, or reference axis can define rotation when supported by the command. The axis must be deliberately located. A diameter describes the distance across a round shape; the distance from the axis to its outer wall is the radius.

A stepped sleeve can often be built with one revolved section including its bore. Repeated extrusions and cuts can also build it, but put the coaxial steps under separate controls. Choose the sequence that makes the important dimensions easiest to change.

Check overlap and axis selection before adding complexity

A section that crosses the axis, overlaps itself during rotation, or creates zero-thickness contact can fail. Simplify the section, verify its closure and axis, and inspect a small-angle preview. For the command entry points see SOLIDWORKS: Sketching with Extruded or Revolved Bosses/Bases. A turned part may suggest a revolve, but CAD history does not specify a lathe process; see [[lathe-operations]].

Pick the base feature from the shape's main organizing rule

Use extrusion when a useful section stays the same along a straight direction. Use revolution when a useful section repeats around an axis. A cylinder can be made either way. A rotationally stepped part often makes the reason for choosing revolve clearer than a plain cylinder does.

Before accepting either feature, confirm the profile, direction or axis, end condition or angle, and result body. Then make one trial dimension change. Does the model still express the intent described in [[feature-based-modeling]]?
