Relations preserve geometry when dimensions change

A geometric relation is a rule the sketch must continue to satisfy: two lines stay parallel, two circles keep one center, or an endpoint stays on a curve. Onshape generally calls these rules constraints; SOLIDWORKS calls them relations.

Choose the entities carefully. Selecting a circle's center is different from selecting its circumference. A relation can remove some motion while leaving other motion available. The diagrams show only the named rule, not a complete dimensioning scheme.

The before/after drawings are original geometry diagrams, not software icons. Their line colors do not indicate sketch status.

Coincident connects a point; midpoint puts it halfway along

Coincident

Two points share a location, or a point lies on a selected line or curve. Joining two endpoints closes their gap. A point on a line can still slide along it unless another rule locates it.

Midpoint

A point stays halfway between a segment's endpoints. It is more specific than simply putting the point somewhere on the line. Onshape also supports midpoint on an arc.

Point selection and vendor names

See Onshape's Coincident and Midpoint. SOLIDWORKS also lists Merge Points for combining sketch points or endpoints in its relation reference.

Collinear shares a line; parallel shares only its direction

Collinear

Two straight segments lie on the same infinite line. They may have a gap between them; collinear does not join their endpoints.

Parallel

Two lines keep the same direction. Their spacing, lengths, and positions along that direction still need other rules.

Software vocabulary: SOLIDWORKS has a Collinear relation. Onshape's documented toolbar has no separate Collinear entry. For two nonzero straight segments, placing both endpoints of one coincident with the other's underlying line enforces the same geometric condition. This is a construction from point-on-line constraints, not an Onshape command name.

Horizontal and vertical use the axes; perpendicular uses another line

Horizontal

A line's endpoints have the same vertical coordinate. For two selected points, horizontal aligns their heights without joining them.

Vertical

A line's endpoints have the same horizontal coordinate. Two selected points can likewise be aligned vertically.

Perpendicular

Two line directions form a right angle. They may rotate together while preserving that angle. The relation does not require their finite segments to meet.

Why horizontal plus vertical can already supply a right angle

A horizontal line and a vertical line already have perpendicular directions. Adding a 90-degree driving dimension can duplicate the existing control. Check the stored rules first. See Onshape: Working with Constraints.

Concentric shares a center; equal shares size; coradial shares both

Concentric

Circles or circular arcs share a center. They can have different radii. For arcs, their endpoints and sweep angles are not determined by this relation.

Equal

Selected line segments keep equal lengths; selected circles or circular arcs keep equal radii. Equal does not move their centers together. Equal arc radii do not imply equal arc lengths when the sweep angles differ.

Coradial

Two circular arcs share both center and radius: they belong to the same underlying circle. They need not touch or have equal sweep angles. SOLIDWORKS names this relation Coradial. In Onshape, concentric plus equal radius expresses this condition.

Check the circular relation definitions

See Onshape's Concentric and Equal. The SOLIDWORKS 2025 relation table correctly places the coradial definition; the 2026 table misplaces its center-and-radius sentence under Perpendicular.

Tangent controls direction at contact; Fix holds a reference in place

Tangent

A line and a circle touch without crossing: the line is perpendicular to the radius at contact. Curves meeting tangentially have a common tangent direction. Tangency alone does not necessarily join the endpoints you intended; add endpoint coincidence when a continuous profile needs it.

Fix

Fix grounds selected geometry at its current location. Fixing a point removes its freedom to move in the sketch plane. It is useful for a deliberate fixed reference, but does not explain why an arbitrary location or size is correct.

SOLIDWORKS qualification: its relation reference says endpoints of a fixed line can still move along the underlying infinite line, and arc endpoints can move along the supporting circle. Check the entity and its endpoints. Onshape describes Fix as grounding the selected entity on the sketch plane.

Vendor help for tangent and fixed geometry

Read Onshape: Tangent, Onshape: Fix, and the Fix row in SOLIDWORKS: Description of Sketch Relations.

Watch automatic relations, then check what was stored

Sketching tools can add relations while you draw. A visual alignment is useful feedback; inspect the resulting relation icons before assuming the intended rule is present.

  • SOLIDWORKS: with Automatic Relations enabled, its documented cursor cues include horizontal, vertical, coincident, midpoint, perpendicular, and tangent. Hold Ctrl while sketching to suppress automatic relations temporarily.
  • Onshape: Automatic Inferencing documents horizontal, vertical, midpoint, parallel, and coincident as common inferences. Hover near existing geometry to activate the relevant inference; Shift suppresses inferencing while dragging.

These are documented examples, not an exhaustive promise for every tool. For equal, concentric, coradial, collinear, or fix, deliberately apply or verify the required rule. Rectangle, slot, and other creation tools can supply their own relations.

If an unwanted rule was stored, remove that specific rule before adding a conflicting dimension. Continue with [[fully-defined-sketches]].
