CourseWiki/Engineering Fundamentals

Engineering Fundamentals

Shared mathematical and technical concepts for describing, calculating, and communicating engineering work.

Units and Measurement

Dimensional Analysis Convert quantities by multiplying by named equalities, cancelling units, and treating squared and cubed units as powers. Measurement Language Use quantities, units, symbols, and SI prefixes precisely before a drawing, program, or calculation has a chance to drift.

Motion and Rotation

Acceleration and Squared Time Calculate change in velocity over time, distinguish average from instantaneous acceleration, and convert squared time units correctly. Angular Motion Measure rotation in degrees, radians, and revolutions, then use angular velocity and angular acceleration to describe a turning shaft. Motion Quantities Distinguish path length from displacement, and speed from velocity, before using time to describe a machine or vehicle in motion. Rotation and Linear Speed Turn a shaft speed into the speed of what it drives, and back again, using the radius - belts, rollers, wheels, and grinding wheels.

Force and Power

Geometry, Mass, and Force Scale CAD geometry correctly, connect volume and density to mass, and keep force and weight as separate quantities. Power and Efficiency Power is work per unit time - three equivalent ways to calculate it, the horsepower conversions, and what an efficiency figure can and cannot be. Torque Turning effort is force times radius - how to calculate it, which unit belongs to it, and why the order of the two words is not decoration. Work and Energy Force moving through a distance is work - the joule, the foot pound-force, and why an energy unit reads backwards from a torque unit.

Electrical Quantities

Current, Voltage, and Resistance The three quantities every schematic and nameplate uses, the units they are measured in, and the two equations that connect them.
