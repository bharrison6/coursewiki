Cutting and Machining Plastics

Choose a separating process for the plastic and the feature, then control heat, chips and edge damage.

Choose the cut from the material and the feature

Choose the cut from the material and the feature

Separating removes material. A saw makes a blank, a drill makes a round hole, and a router or mill can make an outline, pocket or slot. Identify the resin and grade before choosing the process: two clear sheets can be acrylic and polycarbonate, with very different cutting behavior.

ProcessUseful forWhat controls the result
SawingStraight cuts or curved blanks on a band sawUse a blade intended for the plastic and thickness. Teeth must cut without grabbing or packing the cut with chips; the finest tooth spacing is not always best.
DrillingHoles through sheet or solid stockUse suitable drill geometry, support the exit surface and provide chip clearance. Brittle acrylic can crack when the drill grabs at breakthrough.
Milling and routingAccurate edges, slots and pocketsSharp cutting edges, room for chips to escape and a supported workpiece reduce melting and chatter.
Laser cuttingSheet profiles in an approved materialThe resin, additives, thickness and laser wavelength matter. A glossy edge alone does not prove dimensional accuracy.
Hot-wire cuttingContours in suitable thermoplastic foamA heated wire softens a narrow path. This is not a general cutting method for solid plastic sheet or every foam formulation.

See Milling Operations for tool paths and feature access. Machine use belongs with Equipment Operating Procedure and Machine Hazards and Housekeeping.

Make chips instead of rubbing the plastic hot

Make chips instead of rubbing the plastic hot

Heat builds up near the cut because plastics generally conduct heat poorly. Softened chips can stick to the tool or weld back onto the edge. The symptoms are smeared surfaces, strings of softened material and chips packed into the cutter.

Slowing the feed alone can make melting worse. Feed is how quickly the tool advances through the work; spindle speed is how quickly it turns. Too little advance per tooth lets the edge rub instead of taking a useful chip. Start with cutting data for the resin and tool, keep the tool sharp, and maintain chip clearance. Reducing excessive spindle speed may help, but feed, speed and cut depth must be considered together.

Deep holes need a planned way to clear chips. Coolants and cleaners must be compatible with the plastic; an unsuitable liquid can cause fine cracks called crazing. Use Feeds and Speeds for the relationships, not its metal cutting values as plastic settings.

Why a slower cut can produce more heat

A tooth that advances too little spends more time sliding against the work. The next tooth may also recut trapped chips. Useful chips carry some heat away, so reducing chip thickness without reducing rubbing is not a reliable cooling strategy. ACRYLITE's drilling guidance identifies low feed or excessive RPM as causes of melted chips; its routing guidance treats feed, cutting edges and RPM together.

Acrylic, PVC and polycarbonate behave differently under a laser

Acrylic, PVC and polycarbonate behave differently under a laser

Acrylic (PMMA) absorbs the infrared energy of a CO₂ laser. Concentrated heating removes material along the cut and can leave a smooth, glossy edge. That result depends on the sheet grade, thickness and process settings; it is not a promise for every clear plastic or every laser.

PVC is not a laser-cutting substitute for acrylic. Heating PVC can release hydrogen chloride; with moisture it forms hydrochloric acid, creating an exposure and corrosion concern. This is a chemical incompatibility, not simply a poor-looking edge.

Polycarbonate is often a poor choice for a clean CO₂-laser-cut sheet edge. It can yellow, char and leave a degraded edge instead of acrylic's clear finish. Specialized processes can cut some polycarbonate, so “polycarbonate cannot be laser processed” is too broad. Routing or sawing is often the better starting choice for a clear polycarbonate profile. Do not assign PVC's chlorine chemistry to polycarbonate.

Material approval and operating controls belong with Equipment Operating Procedure and Chemicals and Safety Data Sheets. Appearance or a trade name is not enough to identify an unknown sheet.

Manufacturer references and the limits of a material list

Trotec's unsuitable-material guidance lists PVC and explains why composition matters. Boss Laser describes acrylic's cut behavior, PVC decomposition concerns and polycarbonate edge discoloration. Universal Laser Systems' polycarbonate processing guide discusses reducing char through a specialized cutting method. A material name on a capability list does not establish a suitable setup for a particular sheet.

Trim the molding excess without cutting into the part

Trim the molding excess without cutting into the part

A gate is the entry from a molding flow channel into the part cavity. Degating separates the part from that feed connection. Flash is a thin unwanted fin, often at a mold joint; trimming removes it. A burr is a raised edge left by cutting. These are different defects, even when the same trimming tool can remove them.

Support the part near the cut and leave the required contour intact. Cutting into the wall creates a notch that may start a crack. For batches, tumbling with suitable media can remove small burrs, but it can also round wanted edges or damage thin features. Inspect the dimensions and surface after the trial.
