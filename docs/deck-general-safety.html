Getting Permission

Equipment ClassificationEquipment

Three categories, one question

Every piece of equipment in the DET labs is Category 1, 2 or 3. The category answers one question: who has to be present before you switch it on?

Permission from an instructor is required in every case. What changes between categories is when that permission can be given, and who has to be in the room while the machine is running.

Category 1
Permission may be given in advance. Work alone once you have it.
Category 2
Permission at the time of use. A peer must be present throughout.
Category 3
Permission at the time of use. A peer and an instructor must be present throughout.

The category never removes a requirement. General safety procedures and PPE apply to every category, to every person present — see Before, During and After and Personal Protective Equipment.

The Universal Rules

Before, During and AfterUniversal Rules

The procedure that never changes

This applies to any equipment, in any lab, on every use — whatever its category.

Three phases. Nothing in them is optional, and nothing in them depends on what you are making.

DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University8
Before, During and AfterUniversal Rules

Before operating any equipment

  • Obtain permission to operate the equipment from the instructor.
  • Verify training — ensure ALL operators have received proper equipment training.
  • Check PPE — confirm you are wearing all required personal protective equipment. See Personal Protective Equipment.
  • Perform a pre-use inspection — check for any required maintenance or safety issues.
  • Confirm safety guards are in place and functioning properly.
  • Secure the workpiece — ensure all material is properly clamped or held.
  • Communicate your intentions — tell others in the area what you are about to do.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University9
Before, During and AfterUniversal Rules

During equipment operation

  • Stay alert — maintain focus, and never leave equipment unattended while it is running.
  • Follow proper procedures — use equipment only as trained and as intended.

Machine-specific hazards — pinch points, measurement, housekeeping — are covered in Machine Hazards and Housekeeping.

DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University10
Before, During and AfterUniversal Rules

After operating any equipment

  • Power down safely — turn off and disconnect the equipment from its power source.
  • Report issues immediately — notify the instructor of any broken or malfunctioning equipment.
  • Return and organize — put all equipment and tools back in their designated locations.
  • Restore to shutdown state — return the equipment to its original, safe configuration.
  • Clean thoroughly — remove all dust, chips, liquids and debris from the equipment and the work area.

The habit behind the last two is 5S — the same discipline, named and made routine.

Personal Protective Equipment

Five topics. One of them — eye protection — applies the moment you walk in. The rest are set by what you are doing.

Eyes
Always, in the woods and metals lab. No exceptions.
Hearing
Prolonged exposure to loud equipment.
Feet
Closed-toe shoes, always.
Respiratory
Any grinding or sanding; hazardous dust or fumes.
Clothing & hair
Always, near anything that rotates.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University12
Personal Protective EquipmentUniversal Rules

Eye protection

Safety glasses are MANDATORY in the woods and metals lab at all times.

If you wear prescription glasses

  • Side shields that attach to your existing frames.
  • Oversized safety glasses that fit over corrective glasses.
  • Prescription safety glasses are also acceptable.

Lens requirements

  • Safety glasses must be clear or yellow tinted only.
  • Dark or shaded safety glasses are not acceptable for indoor lab use.
  • Must meet ANSI Z87.1 safety standards.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University13
Personal Protective EquipmentUniversal Rules

Hearing protection

Industry standard: shop tools nearly always require hearing protection in professional settings. In this lab the exposure is typically shorter than in industry, but the protection still matters.

When to use it

  • Required for prolonged exposure to loud equipment — grinders, sanders, planers and similar.

Two types available

Earplugs
Small foam plugs inserted into the ear canal. Disposable or reusable. Available on request from the instructor.
Ear muffs
Over-ear cups covering the whole ear. Reusable and adjustable. Often preferred for intermittent use.

Both types significantly reduce harmful noise levels.

DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University14
Personal Protective EquipmentUniversal Rules

Foot protection

  • Minimum requirement: closed-toe shoes are MANDATORY — no sandals, no flip-flops, no open-toe footwear.

Recommended upgrades

  • Slip-resistant soles, for traction on surfaces that may be slippery.
  • Puncture-resistant soles, against sharp metal debris.
  • Steel-toe or composite-toe protection — recommended, not required.

Avoid

  • Canvas shoes or thin-soled footwear.
  • High heels or unstable footwear.
  • Anything that does not fully enclose your feet.

You will be standing for most of the lab period — choose something supportive and comfortable.

Respiratory protection

MANDATORY when using any grinder or sander, and when working with materials that produce hazardous dust or fumes.

Grinding galvanized material is PROHIBITED. Zinc fumes from galvanized steel are toxic when heated.

Health hazards

  • Metal dust and particles cause respiratory irritation and long-term lung damage.
  • Wood dust can cause allergic reactions.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University16
Personal Protective EquipmentUniversal Rules

Respiratory protection — what to wear

N95 mask
Filters 95% of airborne particles. The minimum acceptable standard.
Approved respirator
Half-face or full-face, for extended exposure.

If you can smell fumes or taste particles, your protection is inadequate. Stop work immediately and upgrade it.

Chemicals and liquids have their own documentation — see Chemical Safety and the SDS.

Personal Protective EquipmentUniversal Rules

Clothing and jewelry

Loose clothing and accessories catch in rotating machinery. That is how this causes serious injury or death — it is not a dress code.

Prohibited

  • Loose-fitting clothing, or garments with dangling features — drawstrings, ties and similar.
  • Long sleeves, hoodies or jackets near rotating equipment.
  • ALL jewelry, except silicone wedding rings.
  • Neckties, scarves and lanyards.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University18
Personal Protective EquipmentUniversal Rules

Hair, and what to wear instead

Hair

  • Long hair must be secured back and kept away from equipment.
  • Use hair ties, clips or caps to prevent entanglement.

Recommended

  • Cotton or fire-retardant materials — preferred, not required.
  • Close-fitting, short-sleeved shirts.
  • Pants without excessive loose material.

LiftingUniversal Rules

Safe manual handling

Most back injuries result from poor lifting technique, not from excessive weight.

Team lifting is required for

  • Anything over 40 lbs.
  • Items lighter than 40 lbs but awkward in shape, size or balance.
  • Any object that feels unstable or is difficult to grip.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University20
LiftingUniversal Rules

Proper lifting technique

  • Plan the lift — clear your path and know where you are going.
  • Get close to the object before lifting.
  • Squat down using your leg muscles; keep your back straight.
  • Grip firmly with both hands; avoid awkward hand positions.
  • Lift smoothly using your legs, not your back.
  • Keep the load close to your body throughout the lift.

Setting it down

  • Use the same technique in reverse — squat down, do not bend over.
  • Place objects gently. Do not drop them.

Electrical SafetyUniversal Rules

Inspection — mandatory before each use

  • Check for frayed, cracked or exposed wiring.
  • Look for damaged plugs or connectors.
  • Inspect extension cords for cuts or wear.

If you find damage, discontinue use immediately and notify the instructor.

DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University23
Electrical SafetyUniversal Rules

Safe practice

  • Always disconnect power before performing any maintenance or adjustment.
  • Never yank or pull a cord — grasp the plug, not the cord.
  • Keep electrical connections away from water and wet surfaces.
  • Be aware of overhead wiring and floor-level cords, and avoid contact.

Lockout/Tagout for machinery with rotating parts is covered in Machine Hazards and Housekeeping.

DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University24
Electrical SafetyUniversal Rules

If someone is shocked

Do NOT touch them directly.

  • Turn off the power source first, or
  • use a non-conductive object to separate them from it.

Then follow emergency procedure — call 911 and notify the instructor.

Chemical Safety and the SDSUniversal Rules

What an SDS is

A Safety Data Sheet is required documentation for every chemical and liquid used in the shop.

  • It carries the critical safety information: hazards, handling procedures, first aid and emergency response.
  • It is a legal requirement under OSHA regulations — not a local rule.
DET - School of Engineering - Murray State University26
Chemical Safety and the SDSUniversal Rules

Finding one

  • All SDS sheets are organized in a designated binder for easy reference.
  • Review before use — check the SDS for any unfamiliar chemical or liquid.
  • Ask the instructor if you cannot locate an SDS for a material.

Your responsibility: know the hazards of any chemical you are using, and follow the proper handling procedures.

Machine Hazards and HousekeepingUniversal Rules

Rotating machinery

Gears, pulleys and belts, chains and sprockets are extremely dangerous pinch points.

  • NEVER work on equipment with these features without first disconnecting power.
  • Lockout/Tagout procedures must be followed for maintenance.

Loose clothing, jewelry and unsecured hair are what these features catch — see clothing, jewelry and hair.

5S BasicsLean and Organization

Why 5S sits in a safety module

5S is a lean manufacturing method for organizing a workplace. It earns its place here because most of what it asks for is also what keeps people from getting hurt.

A cluttered bench hides a hazard. An unlabeled container is an unknown chemical. A floor with chips on it is a slip. Every one of those is a 5S failure before it is an incident — which is why the shutdown steps in After operating any equipment are 5S in everything but name.

This page is the basics. 5S will get its own expanded treatment later.

5S BasicsLean and Organization

Sort — Seiri

  • Separate necessary items from unnecessary ones.
  • Remove or relocate items that do not belong in the work area.
  • Keep only what you need for the task in front of you.

  • Organize and label the remaining items clearly, for easy identification.
