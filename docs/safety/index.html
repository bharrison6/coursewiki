CourseWiki/Safety

Safety

The rules that apply in every lab, every day - what to wear, what to check, who has to be present, and what to do when something goes wrong.

Before anything else

Working safely may get old but so do those who practice it

One question, before every machine

Every piece of equipment in the lab is Category 1, 2 or 3, and the category answers one thing: who has to be standing there before you switch it on?

You
A peer
An instructor

Permission may be given in advance. Work alone once you have it.

The full rules, and what each category requires of everyone present, are on Equipment Categories and Supervision. Which machine is in which category is per lab.

If something goes wrong

Injury or fire Call 911
Murray State University Police 270-809-2222
Every incident, injury or near-miss Tell the instructor

Electrical shock is the one case where the first action is different — do not touch the person. See Electrical Safety.

Universal Rules

Chemicals and Safety Data Sheets Every chemical and liquid in the shop has a Safety Data Sheet. Read it before you use the material, not after. Electrical Safety Why 50 V is a workplace control threshold, why 5 V can still burn you, the inspection required before every use, cords on the floor, and what to do when someone is shocked. Equipment Operating Procedure The procedure that applies to every piece of equipment in every lab, every time you use it. Fire Safety When to fight a fire and when to leave, and how to use an extinguisher. Lab Conduct and Emergencies What is never acceptable in a lab, what alertness actually requires, and what to do when something goes wrong. Lifting and Carrying Team lifting above 40 lbs, and the technique that keeps you in control of the load. What usually defeats a lift is the size of the thing, not its weight. Machine Hazards and Housekeeping The two ways a machine injures you - it catches hold and pulls you in, or it closes on you - plus the environment around it and the habits that keep both off you. Personal Protective Equipment What you wear, and when. Eye protection is mandatory in the lab spaces at all times; the rest depends on the task.
