Shop Layout and Angle Measurement

Carry dimensions from a reference onto the work with clear lines, checked centers, and correctly read angles.

Start layout from a reference you can return to

Start layout from a reference you can return to

Layout places lines, arcs, and points on a part to guide cutting or locate features. A neat line is useful only when it is in the right place.

Identify the reference surfaces from which dimensions are taken. Keep using those references rather than measuring each new line from the last one. A layout mark guides the work; it does not prove the finished part is within tolerance.

A surface plate supplies a reference plane for layout and inspection. Clean the plate, the part's supporting face, and the tool base. Support the part so it cannot rock. Use an appropriate angle plate or V-block when the shape needs support.

Protect the plate from impact and abrasive work. Its flatness depends on its size, grade, condition, and calibration; “a surface plate” does not imply one universal accuracy. Follow its specified support arrangement. Level and flat are different: leveling a plate does not repair a worn surface.

Dye makes a fine scribed line easier to see

Dye makes a fine scribed line easier to see

Layout dye adds contrast. Prepare the surface, apply a thin coating as directed, and let it dry before marking. Use Chemicals and Safety Data Sheets for chemical handling guidance.

A scriber scratches a narrow line through the coating. Hold the guide firmly and draw the point along it with light, consistent pressure. Repeated passes can make a wide, ambiguous mark. Scribe only where the drawing and process allow a permanent scratch.

A surface gage carries an adjustable scriber at a fixed height above its base. Set it from a separate height reference, then move the base along the plate to scribe a line parallel to that plane. A surface gage ordinarily has no direct height scale.

A height gage adds a scale or readout. Set its reference correctly, seat the base flat, set and lock the height as intended, and make the line. With a suitable probe it can compare or measure heights. It is not simply another name for a depth gage.

Finding the middle of two heights

If the lower edge of a slot is 0.600 in above the reference and the upper edge is 0.900 in above it, the center is (0.600 + 0.900) / 2 = 0.750 in above that reference. Half the difference, 0.150 in, is half the slot height; it still must be added to the lower height.

Dividers and trammels carry a distance into an arc

Dividers and trammels carry a distance into an arc

Dividers have two pointed legs. Set their spacing from a suitable scale, hold one point at the center, and swing the other to mark a circle or arc. They can also transfer or step off equal distances.

Trammels put adjustable points on a beam for radii too large for ordinary dividers. Lock the points after setting, support the beam, and check the spacing again before marking.

The point spacing is the radius, not the diameter. For a 3.00 in diameter circle, set 1.50 in between points. Recheck transferred dimensions against the original reference; repeated stepping can accumulate error.

Prick-punch the location; center-punch after checking it

Prick-punch the location; center-punch after checking it

A prick punch has a sharper point for a small, precise location mark at intersecting layout lines. A center punch makes a wider depression to help start a drilling operation. Point angles vary by tool; the important distinction here is the fine locating mark versus the wider starting mark.

Locate the point carefully, bring the punch upright, and make a light mark. Check the location before enlarging it. A heavy first mark is difficult to correct. Support the work on a suitable punching support, away from the precision surface plate.

A punch mark is not a substitute for the machine setup or spotting method needed to locate a hole accurately. Check the finished feature with a suitable measuring method. Use Personal Protective Equipment and Equipment Operating Procedure for the applicable shop rules.

Punch types and their uses

Starrett's punch guide explains the sharper prick punch, the wider center punch, and why the locating mark is checked before it is enlarged.

A combination set uses one blade with three different heads

A combination set uses one blade with three different heads

Square head
Provides 90° and 45° reference faces. With the blade it can lay out right angles, check squareness, or establish an offset or depth. Seat the reference face fully before reading or scribing.
Center head
Its two faces seat on round stock, positioning the blade along a diameter. Scribe one line, rotate the work or head, and scribe another; their intersection locates the center on the end face.
Protractor head
Sets the blade to an angle on a graduated head. Read the scale in the correct direction, lock the setting, and recheck contact with the reference.

Clean the mating faces and blade groove so the head seats properly. Pick the tool's accuracy for the task: a combination set is useful for layout, but it does not automatically replace a precision square or a finer angle instrument.

A universal bevel transfers an angle; a vernier protractor reads it

A universal bevel transfers an angle; a vernier protractor reads it

A universal bevel has an adjustable blade and stock that can fit against two surfaces. Lock it to transfer or compare an angle. A plain bevel has no numerical scale; obtain the angle from a separate reference.

A vernier bevel protractor combines the adjustable blade with degree and vernier scales. On a 5-minute instrument, each matching vernier division adds 5 arcminutes. 60 minutes = 1 degree, so 5 minutes is 1/12 degree. This is the reading increment, not a universal accuracy claim.

  1. Seat the stock and blade against the intended surfaces. Identify the angle being measured.
  2. Read the whole degrees between the main zero and the vernier zero.
  3. Use the vernier on the same side and in the same reading direction as those degrees. Find the matching division.
  4. Add the matching division number × 5 minutes to the whole degrees.

Example: 32 whole degrees and the seventh matching vernier division give 32° + 7 × 5′ = 32°35′. That is 32 + 35/60 = 32.5833…°, not 32.35°.

Check the contact geometry before reporting an obtuse angle. If the instrument has measured the acute supplement, a reading of 32°35′ corresponds to 180° − 32°35′ = 147°25′ for the required obtuse angle. Do not automatically subtract every reading from 180°.

Practice bevel-protractor readings

Why the 5-minute vernier works

On the arrangement described in the Starrett catalog, page 306, 12 vernier spaces span 23 main-scale degrees. Each vernier space is 23/12 degrees, or 1°55′: 5 minutes short of two degrees. The line alignment reveals that difference. Page 308 distinguishes a plain universal bevel from the graduated protractor used with it.

Check the layout before making a permanent cut

Check the layout before making a permanent cut

  • Confirm the reference surfaces and dimension directions.
  • Check each line or center from the intended reference, including radius versus diameter.
  • Verify the height or angle after locking the tool.
  • Check the mark before punching more deeply or beginning the cut.
  • Plan how to measure the finished feature; use Choosing Shop Measuring Tools and Tolerances and Fits.

Drawing views and dimensioning conventions belong in the Engineering Graphics collection. Layout follows those requirements; it does not redefine them.
