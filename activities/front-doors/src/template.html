If the model behind the glass is often the same, what actually changes as you move from a chat window to a coding agent?

The one thing to take away

Going inward is not an upgrade. Every door you move down gives the tool more reach and takes away a little of your ability to check its work cheaply — and the cheapest review in the whole table belongs to the agent inside the word processor you already own, because it hands you a change to accept or reject instead of a finished thing to judge.

Which is why the right question is not “what is the most capable tool” but “what is the smallest door that does this job”. Stage 2 answers that seven times.

Guidehow to use this demo

Most people meet AI through a chat box and reasonably conclude that is what AI is. It is one door out of four, and the one that reaches the least.

Where this sits: runs right after The Stranger (Demo 5), which covered how you phrase a request. This one covers what surface you ask it through.

  1. The four doors. Tap any of the sixteen cells for the reason and the source; tap a door’s name for what it is, where it runs and what it costs.
  2. Pick a job. Choose one of seven real faculty tasks, guess which door finishes it, then see the computed answer and what you would hand over.
  3. The receipts. What already changed, what was rechecked and held, and every source, so a stale product name never has to be taken on faith.

What is live

This page runs no model and makes no network request. Every grid cell is one of two things.

Sourced 13 of 16 cells

A page belonging to the company that makes the product was opened on the check date and says this, usually in its own words (quoted where the page allows a clean quote).

Reasoned 3 of 16 cells

The three “you check” cells for the chat window, the desktop coworker and the coding agent are the page’s own ordering judgement about review burden — no vendor ranks how hard its own product is to check.

How it works

reach(door) = max(sees, does, keeps going) — check(door) is read separately

Four axes ask what a door can see, what it can do, whether it keeps going unwatched, and how much you must check. The first three were chosen so the four doors form a ladder of reach, which is why they rise neatly left to right — a property of the selection, not a discovery. Row 4 was not chosen to rise, and it does not: the desktop coworker (door 3) is harder to check than the coding agent (door 4), because doors 2 and 4 hand you a change you can compare and take back (an edit inside a document with undo and version history; a diff you can read line by line) and door 3 hands you a finished artefact with nothing to compare it to.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • The bars are a reading of the four rows, not a measurement of anything — stated on screen in the axis note.
  • “Same model behind the glass” is a simplification: the four doors sometimes run different model versions, but the axis that matters here is the surface, not the model.
  • A door’s reach can change with an institutional license (an admin can turn Gemini Enterprise on) — the page reports what is possible by product, not what your account currently has switched on.

Sources

  • Anthropic — Claude Cowork — the desktop-coworker door’s reach and unattended-work claims.
  • Microsoft — Edit with Copilot in Word — the in-app door’s accept/reject review model.

Boundary: the bars describe reach and review burden as this page’s four chosen doors express them. They are not a benchmark, a market-share figure, or a claim about which vendor writes the best answer.
What is live

28 verdicts, all reasoned from Stage 1’s sourced cells

Reasoned every Stage 2 verdict

No vendor page says anything about eighty student reflections or a syllabus template. Every verdict is this page’s own inference from the sourced Stage 1 capabilities, and each one names the axis that decided it so the inference can be checked rather than trusted.

How it works

bestDoor(job) = the first door, in order 1–4, whose verdict is “yes”

“Smallest” is deliberate: it is not the most capable door, it is the door that finishes the whole job while handing over the least. The seven jobs are checked against all four doors, giving 28 judgements total.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • The seven jobs were chosen to span all four doors, which makes the higher doors look more necessary than a real week actually will (the page says so in its own jobs note).
  • A verdict describes technical capability, not institutional policy — two jobs carry an explicit caution that the real answer depends on your registrar or IT department, not on this page.

Sources

Boundary: a “yes” verdict says a door is technically capable of the job. It does not say your institution licenses that door, or that policy permits it for that data — both jobs that touch student work say so explicitly.

How it works

This page tracks its own accuracy risk rather than hoping it ages well: every product name is dated, and this stage is where drift and confirmation are both reported with the same prominence — understating a still-true claim is treated as seriously as missing a real change.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • “Rechecked and held” means read in an ordinary browser on the date shown, not that it will still be true tomorrow.
  • “One row looked uneven” below records a claim this page got wrong — that Google had no consumer-plan product at door 3 — and the product that filled it, Gemini Spark. The panel now states what was checked, where and on what date, not what does not exist.
