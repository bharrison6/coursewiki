# essay-draft.docx — the student's attached draft (scenario 4)

This text is attached to the prompt in **all eight** lever states of the essay
scenario. It is not part of any lever. Any response that quotes the draft must quote
it **verbatim** from below. The app shows this text in a viewer when the paperclip
chip is tapped, so a presenter can put a quoted sentence on the projector.

Title: **Why Schools Should Start Later**

> Every morning across America, millions of teenagers drag themselves out of bed
> while it is still dark outside. This is a problem that affects almost every high
> school student in the country. Schools should start later in the morning because
> it would be better for students in a lot of ways.
>
> One reason schools should start later is sleep. According to the CDC, teenagers
> need 8 to 10 hours of sleep per night, but about 7 out of 10 high school students
> get less than 8 hours on school nights. The CDC also says that not getting enough
> sleep is linked to obesity, poor academic performance, and mental health issues in
> adolescents. Sleep is very important for teenagers.
>
> Another reason is that teenagers' bodies are different. Dr. Mary Carskadon of Brown
> University has studied adolescent sleep for many years. Her research shows that
> during puberty the body's internal clock shifts, so teenagers naturally fall asleep
> later at night. She calls this a "phase delay." Her research is well known in the
> field of sleep science.
>
> Some people say that starting school later would cause problems with bus schedules
> and after-school sports. It is true that districts would have to make changes. But
> almost any change causes problems at first, and eventually people get used to it.
> So this is not a good reason to keep start times the same.
>
> In conclusion, schools should start later because of sleep, because of teenagers'
> body clocks, and because the problems with changing are not that bad. If schools
> made this change, students would be healthier and would do better in school. It is
> time for schools to do the right thing and let students sleep.

Useful specifics an expert reader would notice (do not all have to be used):

- The thesis sentence — "Schools should start later in the morning because it would
  be better for students in a lot of ways" — names no reason and sets up no structure.
- Paragraph 2 ends with "Sleep is very important for teenagers," which restates the
  evidence instead of connecting it to the claim. This is the rubric's "summarizing
  your sources" problem in its purest form.
- Paragraph 3 is a biography of the researcher; the phase-delay finding is described
  but never linked to a start-time recommendation. Nothing follows from it.
- Paragraph 4 concedes the counterargument and then dismisses it with "eventually
  people get used to it" — a concession with no rebuttal underneath it.
- The transitions are "One reason," "Another reason," "Some people say," "In
  conclusion" — sequence markers, not logical relationships.
- Nothing in the draft says what "better for students" means or how anyone would
  know it happened. No mechanism, no measurable outcome.
