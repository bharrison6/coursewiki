Text becomes action when a loop can call tools

What changes when a model can choose an action, read the result, and continue?

Before you start. Stages 1 and 2 both ended at text. An agent wraps that text in a loop: name an action, let a tool run it, append the result to the context, repeat. The context is the model’s whole working memory and it has a fixed size — which is the constraint this stage is really about.

Takeaway

Tools and a loop turn prediction into action, and the context window bounds what the loop can still see — a run can fail for want of memory, not for want of ability.

Guidehow to use this demo

Follow one idea across three stages: a language model predicts tokens, worked steps can support computation, and an agent loop connects text to tools.

Demo 2 of 17, Part one. Demo 1 handed forward that a model is a representation that can be trained, and ended on the transformer; this demo trains one. It hands forward that a model reads its context (Demo 5) and that a score is a measurement under conditions (Demo 3).

  1. LLM. Read the Era 0 sample, press Train · era 1, and compare eras. In the next-token workbench, change the prompt, era and temperature. Use Tokenize text, Inspect parameters and Trace attention for deeper inspection.
  2. Reasoning. Press Run experiment · train both and compare the live exam results. Then try more frozen-model attempts and Fine-tune on accepted examples.
  3. Agents. Choose You choose the actions or Watch the scripted policy. Change Visible context, restart, and observe what the activity can no longer see.

DetailsLLM

What is live

A small transformer you can inspect

Live every number on this stage

The model is created, trained, sampled and inspected in your browser. The loss, the era samples, the next-token probabilities, the parameter heatmaps and the attention maps are all computed here, on this device, while you watch. Nothing is replayed from a recording.

The page creates an untrained transformer in JavaScript: character embeddings, position embeddings, two attention-plus-feed-forward blocks, normalization and an output layer. Its 42,458 parameters begin as seeded random numbers and update during training.

Training repeatedly asks the model to predict the next character in one small original story. Backpropagation computes gradients; Adam uses them to update the parameters. Inference then uses a selected frozen era to produce next-token probabilities.

How the pieces fit

Tokens become numeric vectors. Causal attention lets each position combine information from earlier positions. Feed-forward layers transform those combined values. The output layer produces one score per possible next token.

Attention weights are real intermediate values from this model. They show a routing pattern for one computation, not a complete explanation of why the model produced an answer.

Sourced the GPT-2 and GPT-3 bars

This page’s own parameter count is computed from the model it just built. The two comparison bars are published figures, cited below. Parameter count alone does not describe training data, architecture, capability or quality — the bars are there for scale, and for nothing else.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • One character per token. Production models use much larger word-piece vocabularies, so the tokenizer here is the simplest honest version of the idea, not a miniature of a real one.
  • One short original story as the entire training corpus. Real models train on corpora many orders of magnitude larger, which is most of why they can say true things and this one cannot.
  • Two attention-plus-feed-forward blocks. The shape of the computation is the same as a large decoder-only model; the scale is not, and scale is not a detail.
  • Training is deterministic here (fixed seeds), so the numbers reproduce exactly. A real training run is not reproducible in this way.
  • Attention weights show a routing pattern for one computation. They are not an explanation of why the model produced an answer, and reading them as intent is the most common way to over-interpret this panel.

Sources

  • OpenAI, “GPT-2: 1.5B release” (5 November 2019) — the 1.5-billion-parameter figure for the largest GPT-2: “we’re releasing the largest version (1.5B parameters) of GPT-2”.
  • Brown et al., “Language Models are Few-Shot Learners” (arXiv:2005.14165) — the 175-billion-parameter figure for GPT-3: “we train GPT-3, an autoregressive language model with 175 billion parameters”.
Boundary: model initialization, training, loss, samples, probabilities, parameter heatmaps and attention maps are computed on this device. What the comparison tests

Direct answers and worked steps

Live both models, both exams

Both models are trained here and both sit their exam here. The scores you read are produced by this page while you wait, not looked up.

Measuredone seeded run, reproducible — not a general result the numbers the guide rehearses

Because the seeds are fixed, this activity produces the same figures every time: 54 out of 96 for direct answers against the worked-step model’s score, the 1/2/4/8-attempt curve, and the four fine-tuning rounds. Those exact numbers are a property of this seeded exercise on three-digit addition. They are not a prediction about any other task.

The two arithmetic models use the same transformer family and share core width, depth and attention-head count. They differ in context length, seed and training steps because their target sequences differ. The result is a measured comparison from this seeded activity, not a guarantee that written reasoning always improves an answer.

The direct-answer model learns text shaped like 347+285=632. The worked-step model learns text that includes column sums and carries before the final answer. Both sit the same fixed exam of unseen generated problems.

Two different follow-up mechanisms

More attempts: the page freezes an under-trained worked-step model, samples 1, 2, 4 or 8 chains per problem, verifies the arithmetic, and selects a verified answer. No weights change during this experiment.

Accepted-example fine-tuning: the activity generates fresh problems outside the model, samples chains, checks both the worked arithmetic and the known generated answer, then performs supervised next-token updates on accepted chains. This is STaR-style self-training. It is not policy-gradient reinforcement learning.

Assumptions and simplifications

  • The task is three-digit addition and nothing else. Every number here is about that task.
  • The two models share core width, depth and head count but differ in context length, seed and training-step budget, because their target text is different lengths. So this is not a perfectly controlled experiment in the format variable alone, and that limit is real.
  • The verifier checks the worked arithmetic and the known answer. It is not a model judging a model.
  • Accepted-example fine-tuning here is supervised next-token training on chains the activity certified. It is STaR-style self-training, not policy-gradient reinforcement learning, and the two are often conflated.
  • The more-attempts activity holds the weights frozen. Whatever it gains is bought with sampling and verification at inference time, not with learning.

Context → decision → tool → observation

Inspect the tool definitions

        
What is scripted

        
The automatic decision policy is a hand-written teaching fixture. It deliberately makes one calculator syntax error, reads the error returned by the real calculator, and retries. The manual mode lets you choose from candidate actions generated by that same scripted policy.

        
What the context experiment shows

        
The selected window controls how much text this activity’s policy can see. When early text falls outside a tiny window, the activity loses access to it. A production agent may also use external memory, retrieval, summaries or saved state, so a context window is not its entire possible memory.

One question for the session

  
What changes when a text predictor is given more structure, more checking, or tools? This page shows three bounded mechanisms: a tiny language model learning next-character prediction, arithmetic experiments with written work and checking, and a tool loop with a scripted action selector. LLM · Language model — 6 minutes

  
Question

  
How can adjustable numbers turn recent text into a next-character prediction?

  
Predict prompt

  
After all four eras, can it do simple math, like 2 + 2? The answer is no: its vocabulary has no digits, so 2+2= reaches it as blank spaces. Have someone type 2+2= into the prompt box to see it. The reveal says so on the card once Era 4 finishes, and Stage 2 trains the same kind of model on sums.

  
Activity

  
0:00
Open LLM. Use Tokenize text and Inspect parameters on an untrained prompt. The numbers begin as random values.

  
1:30
Choose Train the model. Read one untrained sample, then narrate the loss and samples as the first era completes. Use Try next-token prediction to compare likely next characters; change Temperature only after the audience sees the ranked choices.

  
4:30
Choose Trace attention. Select a character and show which earlier positions receive weight for that prediction. It is a live intermediate value, not evidence that the model has a mind or intent.

  
Expected observation

  
Training changes the weights, loss declines, and the model’s next-character distribution changes. Its samples can become more patterned while still being limited by one short, original story.

  
Check-yourself answer key

  
Era 4 writes more like the story than Era 0. What changed? Its parameters. Not a lookup of stored passages (nothing is stored to retrieve — the whole model is 42,458 numbers) and not a bigger vocabulary (Tokenize text shows the same 26 characters at every era).

  
Teaching limit

  
This is a character-level transformer trained from scratch on a tiny corpus. It demonstrates a transformer learning mechanism, not general language ability or a claim about every frontier system.






  
Reasoning · Worked steps — 8 minutes

  
Question

  
How do written intermediate steps and checking change a small model’s behavior on addition?

  
Predict prompt

  
Same exam, which scores higher — answers-only or worked steps? Take the vote before pressing Run experiment; the card echoes each guess and settles it from the two scores on screen.

  
Activity

  
6:00
Open Reasoning and choose Train and compare. Compare direct-answer training with worked column arithmetic. The models share core width, depth, and heads, but their contexts, seeds, and update counts differ to fit their different text formats.

  
8:30
Choose Compare exam answers. Read a live attempt and an exam row. The useful comparison is the presence of written intermediate work, not a universal performance promise.

  
10:30
Optional slow extension: Try more attempts. Run 1, 2, 4, and 8 attempts with the same frozen under-trained model. The arithmetic checker rejects malformed work and accepted chains vote. More attempts add inference-time computation, not training.

  
Expected observation

  
Written steps provide smaller targets for the model to predict. Sampling plus checking can improve the chosen answer even when weights stay fixed.

  
Check-yourself answer key

  
The worked-steps model scores higher. What does that show? That generating intermediate steps gives the same machinery more room to compute. The card’s two wrong options are wrong in opposite directions and both are common in a room: “it understands arithmetic” (too strong — both are next-token predictors, and the fine-tuning is supervised, not reinforcement learning) and “nothing changed, both just predict the next token” (too weak — the two exam scores on the page are the evidence against it). Give both corrections equal time.

  
Teaching limit

  
The checker validates column arithmetic from the addends. The optional accepted-example round also retains chains matching the generated answer, then uses ordinary next-token updates on those examples. That is rejection-selected supervised fine-tuning, not a policy-gradient reinforcement-learning update.

Misconceptions to expect

  
It is just predicting the next token, so it cannot really compute anything.It does only predict the next token — and the worked-steps model still scores measurably higher on the identical exam. Prediction with room to write steps is computation. The two exam scores are the evidence; show them, do not argue it.

  
The worked-steps model understands arithmetic.Same architecture, different training text. It reaches more right answers; nothing on the page supports comprehension, and the fine-tuning is ordinary supervised training on its own accepted examples, not reinforcement learning.

  
Attention shows what the model is thinking.Attention weights are real intermediate values from this computation — a routing pattern for one prediction. They are not intent and not an explanation of why the answer came out as it did.

  
Frontier models are this recipe plus scale.The shape of the computation is the same as a decoder-only transformer. The scale, the data, and much of the training pipeline are not, and scale is not a detail. This page demonstrates selected mechanisms, not a miniature of every production model.

  
The agent got smarter or dumber between the two runs.It is the same scripted policy both times. What changed is what the loop could still see. A run can fail for want of memory, not for want of ability.
