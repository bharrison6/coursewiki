Every widely cited test gets replaced. Why?

MMLU, ARC-AGI-1, SWE-bench, Terminal-Bench 2.0 — each was the standard test for a while and each has been retired or rebuilt. Were they all simply beaten?

Before you start. A test is saturated when scores compress at the top until it stops separating the leaders — on Terminal-Bench 2.0 the top five sit inside each other's error bars. But a benchmark is retired when it stops measuring what it was meant to measure, and being beaten is only one of four ways that happens; the others are bad tasks, contamination and missing coverage. When a successor arrives it often counts something different, so a rising line across generations is not one trend.

Outside the tests: what has AI done, and who checked?

A Nobel Prize, an Olympiad gold, 2.2 million materials, a Millennium Prize Problem — which of these happened the way the headline says, and how would you tell?

Before you start. A benchmark score is a measurement under conditions; an achievement is an event whose truth is settled by human institutions over months or years. So an achievement card carries two things a score never does: its acceptance status — the rung of the ladder from Nobel Prize down to retracted — and the AI's role: a tool people used, a result the system generated and people verified, or a result produced autonomously under stated rules. Read the rung first, then the headline.

Two points do not choose your forecast.

Given everything above, what can two measurements tell you about next year?

Before you start. In stage 1 you filled a gap in published history: the answer existed. A forecast is different — the answer does not exist yet, and the shape of the curve has to be assumed. An invented score below rises from 20 to 30 in one year. Three rules fit both points exactly: a fixed gain, a fixed multiple, and slowing toward a ceiling. Nothing in the two points chooses between them; only the next measurement does. Demo 4 picks this up: history, not a script, is how you judge a boom.

Stage 1 led with magnitude because that is what the evidence shows. These are the limits the same sources report, kept to one sentence each.

    Make the claim specific.

    “This system improved on these tasks, under these conditions” is a claim you can check. “AI can do the job” is not. To plan a change in your own work, choose a representative task and measure quality, time, cost and failures against what you do now.

    Fast benchmark progress and slower work in a particular setting can both be true — the 19%-slower randomised trial sits on the same ladder as the Nobel Prize. Neither result decides the other. The next measurement will, and until then the honest forecast names its assumption.

      How far has AI actually come, how would you know, and what can that tell you about next year?

      Demo 3 of 17, Part two. Demo 2 showed a model being measured; here you read published measurements the same way, and Demo 4 asks whether fast progress means a bubble.

      1. Measure. Drag across the chart to draw where the series went, then press Reveal. Five series; Choose a series jumps between them.
      2. Retire. Press a family under Choose a benchmark family and read how each generation stopped measuring what it was meant to.
      3. Achieve. Filter fourteen real events by acceptance rung and by the AI's role; read the rung before the headline.
      4. Forecast. Drag Years after the second point and watch three rules that fit the same two points diverge.

      Getting around

      The stage tabs switch stages. Details opens the evidence and limits for the current stage. Guide reopens this card; Settings has Open Presenter Notes, Presentation mode, Reset and the data integrity checks.

      Time

      About 25 minutes for all four stages; stage 1 alone is 10. Nothing loads from the network and nothing is stored.

      How it works

      Your drag is resampled onto 64 columns across the shaded region, so a fast swipe and a slow one give the same line. The endpoint is read at the ask date and converted back through the axis scale (linear, or logarithmic for the time-horizon series). The ratio shown is published value divided by your endpoint; the point gap is shown for percentage series.

      Assumptions and simplifications

      • Every series is a selection of published reports, not a leaderboard or a census. Reports that exist and are not shown are absent for teaching reasons.
      • Each series ends where its published record ends, not at the snapshot: the hatched stretch and the dashed rule on the chart mark that, and the label says which.
      • Dates are model release or model-version dates unless a note says otherwise; an evaluation may have been run months later.
      • The five series use different tests, units and evaluators. The order they appear in is a teaching order, not a ranking, and there is no combined score.
      • Vendor-reported figures are labelled as such; where a third party republished or reproduced them, the note says so. No result here was replicated by this page.
      • The drawing activity fills a gap in published history. The ratio at reveal compares your endpoint with one selected report; it does not grade your reasoning, and a bold line that landed near the truth was not thereby a good forecast.

        What is sourced, and what is reasoned

        The numbers and quotations are sourced; the status and route chips are our reading

        Sourced every top score, task count, date and quotation on the generation cards

        Each is copied from the benchmark's own site or leaderboard, the successor's own paper, the lab's own statement, or Epoch AI's run data, and links to it. The Terminal-Bench 1.0 scores are marked not captured because they could not be fetched.

        Reasoned the status of each generation and the route chips

        Saturated, nearly saturated, retired and still discriminating are our reading of the scores and intervals; the four routes — beaten, bad tasks, contamination, coverage — are our classification of the reasons the sources give. Both are shown beside the quotation they rest on so a reader can disagree.

        How it works

        Saturation is read in its precise sense: the top scores sit inside each other's confidence intervals, so the test no longer separates the leaders. A benchmark is marked retired when its authors, its co-author, or the aggregators that carried it stopped reporting it, whatever its top score was. A successor is marked as counting something different when its own documentation says the unit changed.

        Assumptions and simplifications

        • Four families are shown, not the whole field. Multimodal, video and preference-ranking benchmarks open other axes this page does not cover.
        • Task counts for Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 4.0 come from Artificial Analysis, not the benchmark's own page, which does not state them.
        • Two independent evaluators can differ on the same model and benchmark — 46.5% and 59.1% on Humanity's Last Exam for one model. Both are given; neither is chosen.
        • The benchmark-creation rate counts the benchmarks Epoch AI tracks, not every benchmark published.
        • A composite index — one number across many benchmarks — hides exactly this stage's lesson, which is why none is used.

        Sources

          Boundary: that a test was retired is evidence about the test and about the pace that overtook it. It is not evidence that the successor is harder, that progress slowed, or that any two generations can be joined by a line.

              Assumptions and simplifications

              • Fourteen cases are a teaching selection, not a sample of AI achievements, and are weighted toward mathematics because that is where the strongest and the most contested 2025–2026 claims sit.
              • Lean verification proves an argument is internally valid; it does not prove the formalized statement is the one mathematicians set out to prove. That human step is named on the cards it applies to.
              • "Officially graded" for the IMO and ICPC means graded by the contest's own body; no medal was awarded in either case, because medals go to human contestants.
              • The METR randomised trial's 95% interval (2% to 39% longer) is corroborated by secondary sources; METR's own post as read gives the point estimate.
              • The prior kissing-number bound (592) is not stated on DeepMind's AlphaEvolve page and is not claimed here.

                Boundary: the ladder ranks how thoroughly a claim has been checked, not how important it is. A retracted claim and a Nobel Prize can both be about mathematics done by the same kind of system; the rung tells you what has been established, nothing more.

                These are three assumptions, not three predictions. Let t be years after the second point. The linear rule is 30 + 10t. The compound rule is 30 × 1.5t. The saturation rule is 75 − 45 × (9/11)t. All give 20 at t = −1 and 30 at t = 0.

                How it works

                30 + 10t  ·  30 × 1.5t  ·  75 − 45 × (9/11)t

                For this toy score, 100 is the measurement ceiling. An extrapolation above 100 is shown as invalid for that scale rather than clipped. A straight line on a logarithmic axis indicates proportional change; it does not prove that change will continue.

                Assumptions and simplifications

                • The score, its 0–100 ceiling, and the saturation rule's ceiling of 75 are all choices, not fitted facts.
                • Three rules are shown; infinitely many fit two points.
                • The boundary card keeps the limits to one sentence each on purpose. The detailed sceptical reading of each source is in the stage 1 and stage 3 notes; restating it here would let the caveats eat the lesson.
